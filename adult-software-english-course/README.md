# English for Adult Software Developers: From First Sentences to Work Conversations

[中文入口](README.zh.md)

An original course for adults starting English from the beginning. No previous English grammar, pronunciation notation, or course is required. Software experience is useful but not required: technical situations are explained through simple actions.

## What this course can and cannot do

Build the ability to introduce yourself, manage everyday needs, read short messages, ask questions, describe work, and participate in familiar discussions. Listening, speaking, reading, and writing appear together from the first unit.

Completing pages does not guarantee fluency, a CEFR level, or success in every technical meeting. The early material works toward A1-type tasks, followed by A2-type exchanges and selected B1-type workplace tasks. This is curriculum orientation, not certification. Unfamiliar accents, fast group discussion, and complex technical negotiations require further practice.

These are complete text lessons with dialogues, explanations, exercises, answers, and performance tasks. **Recorded audio is not included.** Use the listening lab with a partner, teacher, or text-to-speech reader. Text-to-speech supports initial practice but cannot replace varied speakers or conversational feedback.

## Teaching design and commercial courses

There is no single "New Oriental English course": products for adults, children, exams, and workplace learners can differ. This course does not claim to reproduce a specific current product, teaching schedule, or textbook. All examples here are original.

The design uses common language-teaching practices: progressively harder tasks, clear examples, brief explanations, guided practice, independent communication, spaced review, and assessment with feedback. Grammar supports meaning rather than becoming an entrance examination. Easy repetition and new situations both matter.

## A sustainable schedule

Use **32-48 weeks as a planning estimate**, not a deadline: roughly 5 days of 45 minutes per week, with a 15-30 minute partner or teacher conversation when possible. Some adults need substantially longer. Spend 1-2 weeks per unit, with extra time for foundation work, reviews, and repeated assessments. Do not treat the unit number as a calendar date.

One five-day cycle:

1. Understand the dialogue and vocabulary; listen with text, then repeat short phrases.
2. Learn the pattern, complete exercises, and check answers.
3. Listen without the visible script, answer meaning questions, and replay with text.
4. Do the role-play and writing task; obtain feedback or compare against the checklist.
5. Retrieve earlier material and try a changed version of the current task.

A 45-minute session: 5 minutes retrieval, 10 minutes listening/reading, 10 minutes explanation and guided practice, 15 minutes speaking/writing, 5 minutes corrections. Adjust the balance to the task. If you have 20 minutes, divide the session across two days instead of rushing.

## Safe practice

Use fictional tasks, sample data, and invented names. Do not upload company code, customer information, meeting recordings, or internal documents to external services without permission. Practise deployment and incident language in role-play, not by changing a real system.
