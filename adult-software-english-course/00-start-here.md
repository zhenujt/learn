# Start Here: A Beginner's Study Routine

[中文](00-start-here.zh.md) · [Course](README.md) · [Sounds](01-sounds-and-spelling.md) · [Unit 1](units/01-hello.md)

## What to prepare

Use this course, a notebook, a voice recorder, and a way to hear English: a patient partner, teacher, dictionary audio, or text-to-speech (TTS). No recorded audio is bundled. TTS is a useful consistent model, but later add varied human voices and interactive feedback. A phone is sufficient; no paid service or programming setup is required.

If you cannot understand this page in English, use the Chinese version first. English-only explanations are not an entrance requirement. Learn a little, use it, and return to the reference when needed.

## The first thirty minutes

1. Open Unit 1 in Chinese and understand the situation: two people meeting.
2. Hear `Hello. I'm Mina.` from your chosen voice. Listen once without speaking; then say it in a comfortable short group.
3. Change Mina to your name. Say it without looking. A sentence with your own information is more useful than a perfect copy you cannot adapt.
4. Hear `What's your name?` and answer. Ask the question back. Record both roles if studying alone.
5. Write `My name is ...` and `I'm a developer` or your actual role. Check against the lesson, then close it and write again.
6. Learn the names of the letters in your own name from the sound guide. Do not postpone speaking until you know every sound symbol.

This is a successful first session even if you pause often. Do not attempt a whole grammar reference today.

## What the basic terms mean

| Term | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letter | One symbol in the writing system | b, B |
| Sound | Something you hear and pronounce | the first sound in book |
| Word | A meaningful written/spoken unit | book |
| Phrase | Words that work together | a new book |
| Sentence | A complete message in this lesson | This is a book. |
| Question | A request for information | Is it new? |
| Subject | Who/what a statement is about | I in I work. |
| Verb | Action or state word | work; am |

English normally uses spaces between words, a capital at the start, and punctuation at the end. The pronoun `I` is capitalized. A contraction joins forms: `I'm = I am`; the apostrophe matters. You can type without knowing English keyboard shortcuts.

## A repeatable unit cycle

Spend one or two weeks per unit as needed; later project units may take longer. A lesson is a practice kit to revisit, not a one-time reading assignment.

| Session | Main work | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Understand words, dialogue, and reading; hear the model | A short meaning summary |
| 2 | Learn the pattern; do exercises before opening answers | First attempt and corrections |
| 3 | Listen with text hidden, then check and replay | Notes of information heard |
| 4 | Change details; role-play and answer follow-up questions | A short recording |
| 5 | Write, speak again without the script, review older material | Revised writing and next goal |

Example 45-minute session: 5 minutes retrieval, 10 listening/reading, 10 explanation/guided practice, 15 speaking/writing, 5 correction. On a busy day, do a 15-minute minimum: retrieve two phrases, listen to a short passage, and give a new reply. This maintains contact; it is not equivalent to the full practice session.

Aim for five sessions weekly and an optional 15-30 minute partner/teacher conversation. The 32-48 week course estimate includes foundation work, twenty units, reviews, and reassessment. It is a planning range, not a research-based guarantee of attainment. Restart from your last usable task after interruptions; do not punish yourself with a large catch-up quota.

## How to use answers without fooling yourself

Do the exercise first. Mark `independent`, `with a hint`, or `copied from the model`. All three are useful learning stages, but they are different evidence. After correction, change a name, time, object, or condition and try again. A correct memorized answer does not prove you understood a new message.

Keep an error record with four fields: what I wanted to mean; what I said/wrote; corrected form; a new example. Example: "yesterday" / `I am check yesterday` / `I checked yesterday` / `I checked the page last night.` Choose one or two important errors per session, especially negatives, times, quantities, or ownership.

## A low-pressure baseline

Before Unit 1, try these without studying: say your name, recognize a spoken time, read `This is my laptop`, write one sentence about yourself, ask for repetition. Use the sound guide or a partner for the spoken item. Save what you can do; "not yet" is a valid result. This is not a placement test or a diagnosis.

After Units 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20, use the [assessments](05-assessments.md). Continue when you can complete the important task with manageable support and repair misunderstandings. Repeat a weak activity with changed details; you rarely need to restart the entire course.

## Solo study and feedback

For listening, ask TTS to read only the English script, not the questions or answers. Use [Listening Lab](04-listening-lab.md) for unseen scripts. For speaking, record first, then compare facts and sentence patterns. Speech recognition may mishear accents; its transcript is a clue, not a pronunciation score.

A partner can ask: "What did you mean?", "Which time?", "Is that finished?" Ask them to correct meaning-changing errors first. An AI conversation partner can generate practice turns, but its grammar feedback and factual claims can be wrong. Do not treat an automated score as a certified level.

## Sustainable expectations and safety

The goal is increasingly independent everyday and software-team communication, not accent erasure or memorizing every rule. Listening, interaction, and reading speed need repeated exposure beyond these pages. Learn general English alongside technical contexts so that lunches, travel, and social conversation are not neglected.

Use fictional tickets, accounts, names, and files. Do not share employer code, customer data, tokens, private meeting recordings, or screenshots without authorization. Follow workplace policy before using external language tools. The course's incident examples teach language, not permission to operate real systems.
