# Unit 7: Places and Directions

[中文](07-places-and-directions.zh.md) · [Course](../README.md) · [Next](08-happening-now.md)

## Goal and words

Find a room and describe a location. Words: floor (building level), door, lift/elevator, stairs, left, right, opposite, next to, between.

## Dialogue and reading

A: Excuse me. Where is Room 204?
B: It's on the second floor, next to the lift.
A: Is there a sign?
B: Yes. Go upstairs, then turn left.
A: Left after the stairs?
B: Yes. The room is opposite the kitchen.

Read: **Office map: kitchen opposite Room 204; lift next to Room 204; stairs at the end of the hall. There are two meeting rooms on this floor.** A sketch can help you check spatial relationships.

## Pattern

`There is a room` introduces one thing; `There are two rooms` introduces several. Ask `Is there ...?` or `Are there ...?` Negative: `There isn't ...`.

`in the room` means inside; `on the second floor` identifies a building level; `at the entrance` identifies a point. `next to` means beside, `opposite` means facing across, `between A and B` requires two reference points.

Directions often start with a base verb: `Go straight. Turn left.` These instructions omit `you`. Floor labels differ across countries, so check signs or ask `Which floor number is on the sign?`

## Practice

1. Fill: There ___ two chairs. There ___ a door.
2. Ask whether a lift exists.
3. Translate a location into an English sentence: the laptop is inside the room.
4. Reading: what is opposite Room 204? How many meeting rooms are on the floor?
5. Correct: The room is between the kitchen. (Use the lift as the second point.)

## Listen, speak, and write

Listen and draw a simple diagram without viewing the text. Check it against the dialogue. Have someone change left to right and draw the changed route.

Give a partner directions to an invented room using three steps. Partner repeats the route before "walking" it on paper. Write a four-sentence location message. Do not use a real private address.
