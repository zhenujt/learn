# Unit 11: Experience and Completion

[中文](11-completion.zh.md) · [Course](../README.md) · [Next](12-reasons-and-choices.md)

## Goal and words

Report completion without confusing it with past time. Words: already, yet, still, ever, result, complete, deploy (put a version into an environment), approve.

## Dialogue and reading

A: Have you finished the tests yet?
B: Yes, I've finished the tests, but I haven't deployed the change.
A: When did you finish?
B: I finished this morning.
A: Is the review complete?
B: No. I'm still waiting for approval.

Read: **The team has fixed the login error. Jo tested the fix yesterday. The reviewer has not approved the change yet. Production still uses the old version.** A fix can be complete without being released.

## Pattern

Present perfect: `have/has + past participle`. A participle is a verb form used with a helper here: finished, done, written, sent. It is not always the same as past simple: `write/wrote/written`. Ask `Have you finished?`; negative `I haven't finished`.

Use present perfect to connect past activity with the present question of completion or experience. Use past simple with a finished past time: `I finished yesterday`, not `I have finished yesterday` in standard usage.

`yet` often appears in completion questions and negatives; `already` emphasizes earlier completion; `still` emphasizes continuation. Experience: `Have you ever used this tool?` does not ask for a specific date. British and American preferences sometimes differ; focus on the meaning and clear time information.

## Practice

1. Fill: She ___ finished. I have ___ (write) the report.
2. Correct: I have sent it yesterday.
3. Make negative: We have deployed it.
4. Reading: is the fix in production? What approval is missing?
5. Ask whether someone has used a tool before.

## Four-skill task

Listen and write separate statuses for tests, deployment, and review. Do not reduce all three to "done". Repeat with a different negative.

Role-play a handover: one task is complete, one is ongoing, one is not started. Partner asks when the complete task ended. Write a four-sentence update that distinguishes all three, using a past time once.
