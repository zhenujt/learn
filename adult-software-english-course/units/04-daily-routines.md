# Unit 4: Daily Routines

[中文](04-daily-routines.zh.md) · [Course](../README.md) · [Next](05-questions-and-clarification.md)

## Goal and words

Describe habits, not just identity. Words: work, start, finish, check, walk, usually (most times), sometimes (on some occasions), every day.

## Dialogue and reading

A: What time do you start work?
B: I start at nine. I usually check my messages first.
A: Do you work at home?
B: Yes, I do. My teammate works in the office.
A: Does she start at nine, too?
B: No, she doesn't. She starts at eight.

Read: **On weekdays, Omar walks to work. He checks email at nine. He doesn't work on Sunday. He sometimes cooks dinner with a friend.** Which activity is not about software?

## Pattern

Use a base verb with I/you/we/they: `I work`. Add `s` or `es` with he/she/it: `She works`, `He watches`. `study` becomes `studies`; `have` becomes `has`.

For ordinary action questions, use `Do you work ...?` or `Does she work ...?` After does, return to the base verb: not `Does she works?` Negatives: `I don't work`, `She doesn't work`. Do not add `am` to `I work`.

Usually goes before an ordinary verb (`I usually start ...`) but after be (`I am usually ready`). Listen for the final sound in `works`; it carries information even when quiet.

## Practice

1. Fill: He ___ (check) email. We ___ (start) at nine.
2. Make negative: She works on Sunday.
3. Correct: Does he starts at eight?
4. Reading: how does Omar travel? Does he work on Sunday?
5. Write a question about a colleague's normal start time.

## Listen, speak, and write

Listen to the dialogue with the script hidden. Record the two start times and locations. Ask for repetition if needed. Then change `nine` to `ten` and identify the changed fact.

Interview a partner about three habits and report one using he/she/they as appropriate. Write a five-sentence routine including one negative and one frequency word. A solo learner can invent a second role, but should later try an unpredictable partner reply.
