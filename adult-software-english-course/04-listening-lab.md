# Listening Lab: Ten Short Information Tasks

[中文](04-listening-lab.zh.md) · [Course](README.md) · [Sounds](01-sounds-and-spelling.md) · [Assessments](05-assessments.md)

## Audio setup and boundaries

These are original scripts, not included audio recordings. Ask a partner or teacher to read the English inside a reader card, or use TTS. The learner should see the questions but not the card before listening. A helper can copy only the English script into a speech reader; it must not read section labels, translations, or answers. If studying alone, prepare speech first, close the script, and listen later. If you already read the script closely, count that attempt as supported practice, not an unseen listening test.

Use a comfortable natural voice with clear phrase boundaries. Beginners may need slower delivery, but avoid spelling out every word unless asked. A second voice is useful once the message is understood. TTS can pronounce identifiers oddly and may not simulate natural interruptions; human interaction remains important.

## The listening loop

1. Preview the situation and questions; learn only the small support vocabulary given.
2. Listen once for the situation or purpose. Do not stop to translate every word.
3. Listen again for names, numbers, actions, negatives, and conditions. Write short notes in either language.
4. Ask for a specific repetition, such as `Could you repeat the time?` A reader can repeat the relevant sentence; a solo learner can replay it.
5. Open the script, find exactly where understanding broke, and connect sound to meaning. Was the problem an unknown word, missed boundary, wrong number, or false assumption?
6. Hear the passage again without reading. Give a new spoken reply, then a short written summary.

Record whether each answer was independent, obtained after repetition, or obtained only from text. Repetition is a legitimate skill, not automatic failure. After you learn a card, ask for the supplied variant; the new spoken details become the answer key for that variant.

## Lab 1: A new colleague (after Unit 2)

Support: tester = someone who checks software; bag = a container you carry.

Listen for: 1. Speaker's name. 2. Job. 3. Whose laptop this is. 4. Where the charger is.

<details>
<summary>Reader card 1</summary>

Hello. My name is Rosa. I'm a tester. This is my laptop. It isn't new, but it is ready. My charger is in my bag. The new screen is Kim's, not mine.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Rosa. 2. Tester. 3. Rosa's. 4. In her bag. The screen belongs to Kim; the laptop is not new. Reply: `Hi, Rosa. I'm ... Nice to meet you.` Write: `Rosa is a tester. Her charger is in her bag.`

</details>

Variant for the reader: change tester to designer and bag to desk. The learner must update job and location, not repeat the first answers.

## Lab 2: A changed meeting time (after Unit 4)

Support: changed = became different; instead = replacing the earlier choice.

Questions: 1. Which day? 2. Old and new times? 3. Which room? 4. Which time zone?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 2</summary>

The meeting is on Thursday. The time has changed. It is not at nine fifteen. It is at nine fifty instead. That's five-zero, not one-five. We are in Room 306. All times are UTC plus zero. Please confirm the new time.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Thursday. 2. Old 9:15; new 9:50. 3. Room 306. 4. UTC+0. Reply: `Thursday at 9:50, UTC+0, in Room 306. Is that correct?` `has changed` is a phrase here; Unit 11 explains the form. Do not infer morning or evening if you need that distinction; ask.

</details>

Variant: change the new time to 10:15 and room to 308. Ask for a read-back of all appointment details.

## Lab 3: An unavailable menu item (after Unit 6)

Support: soup = liquid food; total = all prices added.

Questions: 1. Which item is unavailable? 2. What does the customer order instead? 3. Which drink? 4. Total and payment method?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 3: two roles or one reader with pauses</summary>

Server: What would you like?

Customer: I'd like some soup, please.

Server: Sorry, we don't have any soup today. We have rice and sandwiches.

Customer: A sandwich, then. And a small bottle of water.

Server: That's seven dollars in total.

Customer: Can I pay by card?

Server: Yes, you can.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Soup. 2. A sandwich. 3. Small bottle of water. 4. Seven dollars; card is accepted. Say the final order back without mentioning soup as an ordered item. Write: `One sandwich and a small bottle of water, please.`

</details>

Variant: sandwich unavailable, customer chooses rice and water, total six dollars. Change the entire exchange coherently, not only one word.

## Lab 4: Temporary location and current action (after Unit 8)

Support: repair = fix; result = information from a test.

Questions: 1. Where does the person usually work? 2. Where are they today? 3. What are they doing? 4. When should you call again?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 4</summary>

I usually work in Room 12, but someone is repairing the window there. Today I'm in Room 14, opposite the kitchen. I'm running a test now. The result isn't ready yet. Please call me again at two thirty this afternoon, local time.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Room 12. 2. Room 14, opposite kitchen. 3. Running a test; result not ready. 4. 2:30 p.m. local time. In cross-zone work, ask whose local time. Reply: `You're in Room 14 today. I'll call at two thirty. Do you mean your local time?`

</details>

Variant: Room 14 becomes Room 16 and two thirty becomes three thirty; keep the normal room unchanged.

## Lab 5: A failed first attempt (after Unit 9)

Support: connection = link between systems; address = destination identifier here.

Questions: 1. What failed and when? 2. Did restarting solve it? 3. What change helped? 4. What happened after the successful test?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 5</summary>

Yesterday at eleven, the app lost its connection. I restarted it, but the connection still failed. Then I checked the settings. The server address was wrong. I corrected the address and ran the test again. This time it passed. I sent the result to Luis before lunch.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. App connection, yesterday at eleven. 2. No. 3. Correcting the server address. 4. Result sent to Luis before lunch. Tell the story in four steps. Do not report restart as the successful fix. `lost` is the past of lose; `ran` the past of run.

</details>

Variant: the address is correct and a missing setting is added instead. The reader must revise the cause and fix sentences together.

## Lab 6: Finished work and conditional plans (after Unit 12)

Support: permission = authorization; summary = short account of main points.

Questions: 1. What is already complete? 2. What is not complete? 3. Who supplies the dependency? 4. What is promised today, and what is conditional?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 6</summary>

I've written the summary and sent it to the team. I haven't run the final tests because I still need permission to use the new environment. Mei is checking that permission. If I get access tomorrow morning, I expect to finish the tests by Friday. I'll post a status update at four today, UTC plus zero, whether or not I have access.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Summary written and sent. 2. Final tests. 3. Mei is checking permission. 4. Update today 16:00 UTC+0; estimated Friday finish depends on access tomorrow morning. Reply: `The summary is done, but final testing is blocked by access. You'll update us at four today.` Do not turn the estimate into a guarantee.

</details>

Variant: Mei becomes Arun and access becomes available only Friday morning; the reader replaces the finish estimate with `I'll revise the estimate after I get access`.

## Lab 7: A reproducible bug (after Unit 14)

Support: affected = experiencing the problem; session = one period of using the app.

Questions: 1. Environment and browser? 2. Trigger steps? 3. Expected versus actual? 4. Frequency and untested scope?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 7</summary>

I'm testing in the staging environment with Browser R version eight. I open a new note, type a title, and click Save. I expect the title to remain visible, but it disappears after saving. This happened in two of four attempts, each in a new session. I haven't checked other browsers. The note body remains visible, so please don't describe the whole note as missing.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Staging, Browser R 8. 2. Open new note, type title, Save. 3. Title should remain but disappears; body remains. 4. Two of four new-session attempts; other browsers untested. Write a factual title: `Title disappears after saving in two of four test sessions.` Do not claim confirmed database loss. Browser R is fictional.

</details>

Variant: four of four attempts and body also disappears; revise both observed scope and frequency, but keep root cause unknown.

## Lab 8: Review priorities (after Unit 16)

Support: approval = permission to proceed; wording = words used in a message.

Questions: 1. Required behavior change? 2. What must remain unchanged? 3. Which test is needed? 4. Which suggestion is optional?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 8</summary>

The empty-input check needs to happen before the network request. That's required before I approve this change. Please keep the current error wording and add a test to confirm that no request is sent for empty input. I also suggested renaming the helper, but that is optional. You can leave the name as it is in this review.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Check empty input before request. 2. Error wording. 3. Confirm no request for empty input. 4. Helper rename. Reply: `I'll move the check and add the test while keeping the message. I'll leave the rename for now.` Approval is not already given.

</details>

Variant: rename is required too; reviewer still allows message wording to remain. Learner must not reuse the old optional classification.

## Lab 9: A release decision meeting (after Unit 17)

Support: target = desired outcome; evidence = information supporting a decision.

Questions: 1. Is release approved? 2. What evidence is missing? 3. Who posts it and when? 4. Who decides and when?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 9</summary>

Coordinator: Friday is our target, but we haven't approved the release.

Developer: The small-file tests passed. The large-file result is still pending.

Coordinator: Omar, can you post that result by sixteen thirty today, UTC plus zero?

Omar: Yes. If the test is still running, I'll post that status instead.

Release owner: I'll review the evidence tomorrow at ten, UTC plus zero, and make the release decision then.

Coordinator: Thanks, Leila. I'll record you as the decision owner.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. No; Friday target. 2. Large-file test result. 3. Omar, today 16:30 UTC+0, status even if no result. 4. Leila, tomorrow 10:00 UTC+0. Produce three action notes, separating the test update from the release decision.

</details>

Variant: change review to tomorrow 11:00 and add `Friday is no longer our target; we will choose a new date after review`. Revise the earlier Friday statement too.

## Lab 10: Incident handover (after Unit 20)

Support: sample = limited set examined; recovery = return to normal service; handover = transfer of responsibility.

Questions: 1. What impact is confirmed and where? 2. What does the retry prove? 3. What remains unknown? 4. Who owns the next update and at what time? 5. What should not be announced yet?

<details>
<summary>Reader card 10</summary>

Here's the handover. At thirteen ten, four of six report downloads failed in our test environment. One retry succeeded at thirteen twenty, but we haven't confirmed recovery. We also haven't checked production, so its impact is unknown. The download service logs are being reviewed. No cause or workaround has been confirmed. Chen will take over the investigation at fourteen hundred and post the next update at fourteen thirty, UTC plus zero. Please don't announce that the service is restored based on the single successful retry.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answers and response</summary>

1. Four of six test downloads at 13:10. 2. That one retry succeeded, not general recovery. 3. Production impact, cause, workaround, broad recovery. 4. Chen takes over 14:00, updates 14:30 UTC+0. 5. Confirmed restoration. Give a 45-second read-back and write a concise handover. This is language practice, not operational training.

</details>

Variant: production checks confirm the same problem, but cause and recovery remain unknown. Change only the evidence affected by this new observation.

## After the ten labs

Use the [assessments](05-assessments.md) with fresh scripts. For continued listening, choose lawful short materials with transcripts and topics you partly understand: everyday dialogues, beginner explanations, then public software demos. Listen first, check the transcript, replay, summarize, and respond. Increase length, speed, speaker variety, or topic novelty one at a time; difficulty is not determined by duration alone.

Do not use confidential workplace recordings without authorization. Captions and transcripts can assist learning but may contain errors. Gradually include text-hidden attempts and live follow-up questions so that reading ability does not conceal a listening gap.
