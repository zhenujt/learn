# Assessments: Five Checks of What You Can Use

[中文](05-assessments.zh.md) · [Course](README.md) · [Listening practice](04-listening-lab.md) · [Grammar help](02-grammar-reference.md)

## How to assess fairly

Use Checks 1-5 after Units 4, 8, 12, 16, and 20. These are course progress checks, not standardized CEFR exams. Allow roughly 30-45 minutes for early checks and 45-60 for later ones, split across sessions if useful. The time is a planning guide, not a speed requirement.

Do a first attempt without models, translation tools, or the answer key. Read the learner questions; ask a partner to read only the hidden English listening card. The reader may repeat a sentence on request. Record repeats and hints rather than silently treating them as independent performance. If you must prepare TTS yourself and see the card, mark the attempt as supported; use a changed version for an unseen check later. No audio files are bundled.

For reading comprehension, short answers in either language are acceptable: this isolates understanding from writing skill. For speaking and writing tasks, use English. Record speech with permission. A solo role-play can check production, but it does not verify real-time interaction with an unfamiliar speaker; arrange a partner attempt when possible.

## A shared rubric, not a magic passing score

| Dimension | 0: not demonstrated | 1: emerging | 2: usable with support | 3: independently usable in this task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Essential message absent or reversed | Fragments; major facts need correction | Main facts clear after a repair | Facts, limits, and intended action clear |
| Interaction | Cannot respond to the task | Mainly recites; relies on supplied replies | Asks or answers with pauses/repetition | Handles changed details and repairs misunderstanding |
| Language and intelligibility | Listener cannot recover the message | Frequent form/sound problems obscure it | Errors remain but key meaning is understandable | Patterns and pronunciation reliably convey this message |
| Listening and reading | Main information not recovered | Some details; major misunderstanding | Main information recovered with targeted support | Main points and crucial details recovered without text substitution |
| Writing usefulness | No usable message | Missing essential facts or organization | Reader can act after limited clarification | Reader can act; facts, owner, and time clear where relevant |

Write one piece of evidence beside each rating. Do not convert the total to a CEFR label. Progress when the essential task works, remaining support is manageable, and you can correct a misunderstanding in a changed version. If one skill has no evidence, record it as untested, not passed.

Treat these as repair priorities regardless of other strengths: wrong time or owner, missed negative, invented approval, unverified cause presented as fact, missing safety-critical limitation. Correct the meaning, repeat the weak activity with new details, and then move on. Accent difference alone is not a failure.

## Check 1: Introductions, times, and routines (after Unit 4)

### Listening

Questions: 1. Name and job? 2. Normal start time? 3. Friday start time? 4. Where is the laptop? Ask for spelling if needed.

<details>
<summary>Reader-only card 1</summary>

Hi. I'm Eli. E-L-I. I'm a designer. I usually start work at eight thirty. On Friday, I start at nine. This is my desk. My laptop is on the desk, and my charger is in my bag.

</details>

### Reading

**Noor is a developer. She works at home on Monday. She starts at ten. Her teammate, Alex, works in the office and starts at nine. The team call is on Monday at 11:15 a.m. UTC+0.**

Questions: 1. Where does Noor work on Monday? 2. Who starts earlier? 3. When is the call, including zone?

### Speaking and writing

Meet a new colleague. Greet them, say your name and role, ask their name and start time, and describe one object that belongs to you. Partner card: your name is Val, you start at 8:45, and the learner initially hears 8:15. Correct them; the learner must confirm the right time. Then ask where they usually work. Use fictional personal details freely.

Write four to six sentences introducing yourself and your routine, with one negative and one ownership phrase. Use the grammar you know; do not add long translated sentences.

### Language repair

1. I developer. 2. She start at nine. 3. Does you work here? 4. These is my screens.

<details>
<summary>Check 1 key and examples</summary>

Listening: Eli, designer; usually 8:30; Friday 9:00; laptop on desk. Reading: at home; Alex; Monday 11:15 a.m. UTC+0. Morning/evening for Eli's start is not explicitly stated; do not demand more than the card supplies.

Repairs: I am a developer. She starts at nine. Do you work here? These are my screens.

Writing model: My name is An. I'm a developer. I work at home. I usually start at nine. I don't work on Sunday. This is my laptop.

Speaking evidence: partner can identify name, role, ownership, and correct time; learner can ask and respond, not only introduce themselves. Sample repair: Eight forty-five, not eight fifteen. Is that right?

</details>

Remediation: confused am/is/are or ownership -> Units 1-2; number error -> sound guide and Unit 3; routine questions -> Unit 4. Retest with a different name and 10:15/10:50 rather than repeating Eli's details.

## Check 2: Everyday needs and current activities (after Unit 8)

### Listening

Questions: 1. What drink is unavailable? 2. What does the customer choose? 3. Quantity and total price? 4. Is cash accepted?

<details>
<summary>Reader-only card 2</summary>

Customer: Two coffees, please.

Server: Sorry, we don't have coffee today. We have tea and water.

Customer: Two teas, then. How much are they?

Server: Six dollars in total.

Customer: Can I pay in cash?

Server: Yes, cash is fine.

</details>

### Reading

**Jules usually works in Room 10. Today Jules is using Room 18 because Room 10 is closed. Room 18 is next to the stairs and opposite the kitchen. Jules is checking a report now and is not available until 2 p.m. local time.**

Questions: 1. Usual and current rooms? 2. Two location clues? 3. Current activity? 4. Available now? Is the time zone specified precisely enough for someone in another country?

### Speaking and writing

You need to find Room 18 and ask a colleague for help. Partner gives one instruction you miss: `Turn right after the stairs`. Ask them to repeat the direction, read it back, and ask whether they are free now. Partner says they are checking a report and can help at three. Confirm the time and whose local time it is.

Write a short message of five to seven sentences: your usual location, today's location, directions to it, current activity, and a request for a later call. Include the agreed zone or explicitly ask for it. Separately say a simple two-item food order and respond when one item is unavailable.

### Language repair

1. I am usually work here. 2. She checking the report now. 3. There is two rooms. 4. Could you to repeat that?

<details>
<summary>Check 2 key and examples</summary>

Listening: coffee unavailable; two teas; six dollars total; cash accepted. `Two teas` is normal ordering shorthand for two cups of tea. Reading: Room 10/18; next to stairs, opposite kitchen; checking a report; not available now; local time needs clarification across zones.

Repairs: I usually work here. She is checking the report now. There are two rooms. Could you repeat that?

Writing model: I usually work in Room 10. Today I'm in Room 18. It is next to the stairs. Turn right after the stairs. I'm checking a report now. Could we talk at three? Do you mean your local time?

Speaking evidence: learner asks about a missed direction and preserves the new appointment rather than reusing the reading's 2 p.m. Order example: I'd like rice and water, please. If rice is unavailable: A sandwich instead, please. How much is it?

</details>

Remediation: vague clarification -> Unit 5; quantity/order -> Unit 6; spatial confusion -> Unit 7; habit/current activity confusion -> Unit 8. Retest with a different room, direction, and activity.

## Check 3: Past events, completion, and choices (after Unit 12)

### Listening

Questions: 1. What was completed yesterday? 2. What is not finished? 3. What dependency affects the estimate? 4. Is Tuesday guaranteed? 5. What is promised at noon?

<details>
<summary>Reader-only card 3</summary>

Yesterday I updated the settings and sent the notes to Ren. I've finished the notes, but I haven't finished testing. I need a new test account. If I get it on Monday morning, I expect to finish by Tuesday. I'll confirm the estimate at noon on Monday, UTC plus zero, even if I still don't have access.

</details>

### Reading

**We need a simple report by Wednesday. Option A takes one day and includes a table. Option B takes four days and includes charts as well. We can start on Monday. Charts are useful but not required for this task. Both options need approved data. Approval is still pending.**

Questions: 1. Which option better fits the stated deadline, assuming access is ready at the start? 2. What extra does B provide? 3. What could block either choice? 4. Can the writer truthfully say work is approved now?

### Speaking and writing

Give a 60-90 second update: yesterday you wrote tests; today you are running them; final results are pending. Partner asks when you can finish. Request missing access and give a conditional estimate. Partner then says access will be two days later than expected. Revise the estimate or promise to recalculate it; do not repeat the old date as certain.

Write 60-90 words choosing a report option from the reading. Include the reason, limitation, dependency, and next update. Word count guides scope, not a pass/fail rule.

### Language repair

1. Did you sent it? 2. I have finished it yesterday. 3. If access will arrive, we can start. 4. Option A is more faster.

<details>
<summary>Check 3 key and examples</summary>

Listening: settings updated and notes sent yesterday; testing incomplete; new test account Monday morning; Tuesday conditional estimate; confirm estimate Monday noon UTC+0, not necessarily finish testing. Reading: A; charts; data approval; no.

Repairs: Did you send it? I finished it yesterday. If access arrives, we can start. Option A is faster.

Writing model: I recommend option A because we need the report by Wednesday. It takes one day, so it fits the schedule better than option B if we can start on Monday. It provides a table but no charts. Charts are not required for this task. Both options depend on approved data, and approval is still pending. I'll check the approval status and send an update on Monday morning.

Speaking evidence: all three time/status categories are distinct; access delay causes a meaningful revision. Example: That changes the plan. I'll check the remaining work and revise the estimate after we confirm access.

</details>

Remediation: action order -> Unit 9; promise versus estimate -> Unit 10; completed versus ongoing -> Unit 11; comparison and condition -> Unit 12. Retest with a new dependency and a different option trade-off.

## Check 4: Bugs, requirements, and reviews (after Unit 16)

### Listening

Questions: 1. Allowed type and number of files? 2. Limit and whether it includes the boundary? 3. Above-limit behavior? 4. Which issue is still undecided?

<details>
<summary>Reader-only card 4</summary>

For this version, users can upload one CSV file at a time. The maximum size is five megabytes, and a file exactly at the limit is allowed. A larger file must show an error and must not be uploaded. Multiple files are excluded. We haven't decided whether an empty file should be accepted. Please confirm that with the requester before implementing the empty-file case.

</details>

### Reading

**Bug UP-9: test environment, Browser S 4. Select one CSV file above the agreed size limit and click Upload. Expected: error; upload blocked. Actual: upload begins and no error appears. Observed in both of two attempts. Other browsers not checked. Review: correcting the size check and adding an above-limit test are required. Renaming the helper is optional.**

Questions: 1. Reproduction steps? 2. Which requirement is violated? 3. Known frequency and untested scope? 4. Is renaming necessary for this review? Is approval already given?

### Speaking and writing

Explain the bug to a partner who asks about the browser, frequency, and unknown cause. Do not claim all users are affected. Then clarify the empty-file requirement: the requester has not decided, so assign a follow-up rather than inventing acceptance. Discuss the required fix with a reviewer; raise a concern about preserving the existing error wording and agree on a test.

Write a 70-110 word bug report or review reply with environment, observation, required next step, unknown scope, and a request. State the evidence without copying the entire reading.

### Language repair

1. It happens when I clicks Upload. 2. Could you clarify what should we do? 3. I suggest to add a test. 4. Users don't have to upload large files. (Intended meaning: large files are forbidden.)

<details>
<summary>Check 4 key and examples</summary>

Listening: one CSV; 5 MB inclusive; error and no upload above limit; empty-file acceptance undecided. If byte-level definition matters, ask; the script does not supply it. Reading: select above-limit CSV, Upload; size constraint and blocked-upload behavior; two of two, other browsers untested; rename optional, approval not supplied.

Repairs: It happens when I click Upload. Could you clarify what we should do? I suggest adding a test. Users must not upload files above the limit. (The system must enforce the stated restriction.)

Model: In the test environment on Browser S 4, an above-limit CSV begins uploading without an error. I reproduced this in both of two attempts by selecting the file and clicking Upload. The agreed behavior is to reject it and show an error. Other browsers have not been checked, and the cause is not confirmed. I'll correct the size check and add the required test while preserving the current error wording. Could the requester also confirm whether empty files are allowed? I'll leave the optional rename unchanged.

Speaking evidence: learner identifies facts, unknowns, required changes, and a genuine clarification question. A plausible guessed root cause is not accepted as established evidence.

</details>

Remediation: progress/request -> Unit 13; reproduction and evidence -> Unit 14; boundaries and optionality -> Unit 15; feedback and response -> Unit 16. Retest with a different file type and a missing-field bug.

## Check 5: A new integrated workflow (after Unit 20)

### Reading: a new brief

**Feature: invitation links for a fictional team workspace. A user with the admin role can create one link valid for 24 hours. Ordinary members cannot create links. Expired links must show an error and must not add a member. Bulk invitations are out of scope. The supported browser list and how to display the expiration time are still open. Niko reviews the requirements. Sara owns test accounts. Monday is a target, not an approved release date.**

Questions: 1. Who can create a link? 2. What must an expired link do and not do? 3. Name two open questions. 4. Is Monday approved? These facts define the language exercise, not a full security design.

### Listening: a project update

Questions: 1. Which part of testing passed? 2. What failed? 3. What is still blocked? 4. Who acts at each stated time? 5. Is the release approved?

<details>
<summary>Reader-only card 5</summary>

The valid-link test passed in Browser T version six. The expired-link test failed twice: it showed an error, but it still added the member. We haven't checked the ordinary-member permission case because the test account is missing. Sara will provide that account tomorrow at nine, UTC plus zero. Niko will review the evidence at eleven tomorrow, in the same time zone. Monday remains a target. We must not describe the expired-link behavior as correct just because an error appeared.

</details>

### Speaking: meeting, demo, and changed information

Prepare from five keywords, not a full script. Explain the feature for one minute using paper screens if needed. Then discuss the failed and missing tests for two minutes. Ask one requirements question and request the missing account. Partner asks: `Can we release on Monday?`, `Do all browsers fail?`, and `What does an expired link do now?`

After the first exchange, the partner supplies new information: `Sara says the account will not be ready until tomorrow at noon, UTC+0. Niko's eleven o'clock review is still scheduled.` Explain the conflict and propose a revised review arrangement or an explicitly partial review. Ask for confirmation; do not silently move someone else's meeting or claim the missing test will be ready at eleven.

### Writing: handover and incident-style note

Write a 100-150 word handover with purpose, scope, evidence, missing test, revised dependency, proposed next step, and release status. Add a separate three-sentence factual issue update. There is no evidence of production impact or a root cause; label those unknown if discussed. Do not announce recovery.

### Language repair

1. The test was pass yesterday. 2. The link shows an error, so no member was added. (Contradicts the supplied evidence.) 3. Could you confirm when will the account be ready? 4. We have approved the release. (No approval exists.)

<details>
<summary>Check 5 key and handover model</summary>

Reading: admins; show error and do not add member; browser list and expiration display; no. Listening: valid-link case in T 6; expired link adds member despite error, twice; ordinary-member permission case lacks account; Sara tomorrow 09:00 UTC+0 before later change, Niko tomorrow 11:00 UTC+0; no approval. The later card changes account delivery to noon, not the evidence-review time automatically.

Repairs: The test passed yesterday. / The test was passed yesterday is possible in other contexts, but active is natural for a test result. The link showed an error, but it still added the member. Could you confirm when the account will be ready? The release has not been approved.

Handover model: This feature lets admins create one invitation link valid for 24 hours. Ordinary members must not create links, and bulk invitations are out of scope. The valid-link test passed in Browser T version six. However, the expired-link test failed twice: it showed an error but still added the member. The ordinary-member permission case is untested because the account is missing. Sara now expects to provide it tomorrow at noon, UTC+0, after Niko's scheduled eleven o'clock review. Could we move the full evidence review until after that test, or use eleven o'clock for a partial review? Please confirm the arrangement. Browser scope and expiration-time display still need clarification. Monday remains a target, not an approved release date.

Issue note: The expired-link test added a member in both attempts despite showing an error. The cause and production impact have not been confirmed. We need to verify the correction and the missing permission case before requesting a release decision.

Speaking evidence: listener understands purpose and actual failure; learner answers an untested-scope question honestly, recognizes the scheduling conflict, and proposes rather than falsely confirms a new arrangement.

</details>

Remediation: accurate notes -> Unit 17; explaining behavior -> Unit 18; uncertain incident status -> Unit 19; handling connected tasks -> Unit 20. Reassess with a different small feature, different voices, and at least one unseen change.

## Record the result and choose the next step

Keep one row per attempt: date, check number, task, facts correct, support used, communication repair, one improvement goal, date of changed retest. Keep the recording or writing only if privacy permits. Do not label copied model text as your independent output.

After Check 5, select the weakest real skill: more short text-hidden listening for missed details; live question-and-answer practice for interaction; short graded reading for slow decoding; message rewriting for unclear requests. Continue everyday English alongside work scenarios. A qualified teacher or suitable standardized test can give an external level estimate; this course alone cannot certify one.
