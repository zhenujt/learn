# 01: Learning English for Your Actual Work

[中文版](01-成年人如何学好英语.zh.md) · [Learning methods](README.md)

## Start with an observable goal

For a software developer who knows some common words and uses Teams captions, a useful first goal is: understand a short English caption, give an accurate one-sentence reply, and clarify uncertainty. This is a starting hypothesis to test, not a diagnosis of your level.

Reading this guide or studying for a fixed number of weeks does not guarantee B1 proficiency. Familiar workplace exchanges, unfamiliar discussions, and independent listening make different demands.

## Five practical principles

1. **Use existing knowledge.** Familiar words, remembered pronunciations, and software knowledge are useful starting points. Adults can use explicit explanations and work goals; individual progress varies, and comparisons with children do not predict results.
2. **Learn grammar and replies together.** Do not wait to finish grammar before speaking, or imitate everything without understanding. Learn a short pattern, interpret it, and use it in a reply.
3. **Choose understandable material.** Chinese explanations, English captions, and prompt cards can support practice. A tutor may help with feedback, but no particular tool or background exposure guarantees progress.
4. **Accept mistakes, but check meaning.** Minor errors can be tolerated during practice. Negation, owners, dates, conditions, and status need attention because errors can change colleagues' actions.
5. **Measure a specific task.** "I can confirm which ticket to update" is more informative than "I finished a chapter." Use a new example to check transfer, not just a memorized answer.

## A small baseline

Use the five prompts in the [caption practice unit](../04-工作沟通B1/software-meeting-demo-14-day.md):

- Explain each caption, using Chinese if helpful.
- Give a short reply that fits the facts; note whether you need a model.
- When audio is available, listen to a few of the same sentences with and without text.
- Record what caused difficulty: words, sentence structure, sound recognition, or reply formation.

This is a practice diagnostic, not a standardized CEFR assessment. Use it to choose the next exercise without assuming pronunciation or grammar is the only difficulty.

## Balance the four skills

There is no fixed input/output ratio for every learner. For the current caption-supported goal, try the plan's 40-minute allocation: 15 minutes understanding captions, 10 minutes short replies, 10 minutes listening and repetition, and 5 minutes review with changed examples. Adjust based on errors and available time.

Reading supports understanding; listening connects text to speech; speaking practises retrieval; writing a short confirmation makes commitments explicit. Comprehension does not automatically produce spontaneous replies, so practise both.

Keep practice manageable and revisit material across days. A missed day does not erase progress. Difficulty does not guarantee a breakthrough if you persist unchanged: simplify the task, examine errors, or seek feedback.

## Weekly check

Can you understand a new caption, identify the speaker's intent, and reply without changing the facts? Can you ask for clarification instead of guessing? Note whether you used captions, translations, or a prompt card. Reduce support gradually rather than hiding it to improve a score.

Use fictional or permitted workplace examples. Do not upload confidential meeting material to external services without authorization.

## Self-check

1. Must you finish grammar or IPA before practising a meeting reply?
2. Which errors deserve immediate attention?
3. Is a fixed input/output percentage required?
4. How do you choose the next exercise?
