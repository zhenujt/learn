# 03: Listening With English Captions

[中文版](03-听力训练法.zh.md) · [Learning methods](README.md)

## First goal

Understand a short English meeting caption, give an accurate reply, and clarify uncertainty. Independent listening is a longer-term goal, not a prerequisite for supported participation. Chinese translation can help establish meaning, but understanding it alone does not demonstrate English comprehension. Return to the English sentence; you do not need to suppress mental translation or finish IPA first.

## Diagnose the difficulty

| What happens | What to practise |
|---|---|
| A key word is unfamiliar. | Learn meaning and pronunciation in this sentence. |
| Words are familiar but the sentence is unclear. | Identify who does what, state, time, negation, and conditions; consult one grammar point. |
| The text is clear but the audio is not. | Listen to the same sentence with text, then pause and repeat in short chunks. |
| Understanding is too slow. | Shorten the material, preview vocabulary, and vary details in familiar questions. |
| The question is clear but no reply comes. | Practise one truthful reply, then change the situation and reply again. |

Compare text comprehension, listening, and replies rather than assuming pronunciation is the only difficulty.

## Daily loop

Choose 3-5 short fictional or permitted work lines. Include these activities within the plan's daily study time, not as extra homework.

1. Read the English caption. Explain the person, action, state, time, negation, or condition where present. Check a translation if needed.
2. Identify the intent: question, request, suggestion, or report. Give one reply matching the facts.
3. Listen with English text, replay a difficult part, then pause and repeat. Immediate shadowing is optional.
4. Briefly hide the text and listen again. Restore it to check missing information instead of guessing repeatedly.
5. Next day, change the owner, task, date, or negation. Explain the new meaning and reply.

Example: **Can you check whether the tests passed?** This requests a check; it does not say the tests passed. Reply **Yes, I'll check the results.** if you can check, or **Which tests do you mean?** if the reference is unclear.

Use the [caption practice unit](../04-工作沟通B1/software-meeting-demo-14-day.md) for questions with hidden answers. Consult the [grammar guide](../04-工作沟通B1/software-workplace-grammar-guide.md) for the pattern you need.

## Teams precautions

Live captions scroll, can lag or revise earlier text, and may misrecognize names, technical terms, numbers, and negation. Confirm important owners, deadlines, scope, and deployment instructions, using chat when appropriate.

- **Could you repeat the last part?** Ask for missing information.
- **Do you mean this Thursday?** Check the date without promising delivery.
- **Could you put the deadline in the chat?** Request a written reference.
- **Just to confirm, should I wait for QA approval before deploying?** Verify the condition before acting.

Do not record meetings or upload internal captions, code, or customer information to external tools without permission. Use fictional or explicitly permitted material. Edited examples are not verbatim meeting records.

## Materials and support

Start with short work sentences instead of long podcasts you cannot yet follow. If a line remains unclear with text and a brief explanation, simplify it or learn a prerequisite pattern. There is no universal comprehension percentage for choosing material.

Listening to familiar material can add exposure, but background audio alone does not check comprehension or replies. Start with pause-and-repeat; try shadowing after understanding and comfortably saying the sentence. Familiar sentences can still help retrieval and sound recognition.

Keep captions as needed, gradually test a short line without them, and occasionally use a new example or another speaker. Recognizing a memorized Jenny recording does not establish understanding of live colleagues.

## Self-check

1. What should you check when words are familiar but a caption is unclear?
2. Does understanding a Chinese translation establish English comprehension?
3. Why confirm a deployment condition?
4. How can you test more than memorization?
