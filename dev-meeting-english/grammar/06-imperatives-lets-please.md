## What This Lesson Solves

When a host moves a meeting forward or a colleague asks for help, they almost always use the patterns in this lesson. Three of the most common opening lines:

```text
Okay, let me share my screen.

Let's start with the web part.

Please take a look and let me know if you have any comments.
```

None of these starts with "I", "we", or "you". They start with a verb: `let`, `take`. These are **imperatives**. They are everywhere in meetings, but they are rarely orders. Most of the time they **organize the meeting, make requests, or invite people to speak**.

**After this lesson, you can:**

1. Recognize an imperative (a sentence starting with a base verb) and understand its real tone.
2. Tell apart `let's` (we together), `let me` (I will), and `let me know / let us know` (tell me / tell us).
3. Choose between `please`, `can you`, `could you`, and `would you mind` by politeness level.
4. Recognize non-native forms such as `let us to decide` and `can let me share` without copying them.

## 1. Imperatives: Sentences That Start With a Base Verb

### 1.1 Pattern

An imperative speaks to "you", so the subject `you` is usually dropped and the sentence starts with the **base verb**.

**Pattern:**

- Positive: `base verb + rest.`
- Negative: `Don't + base verb + rest.`
- Polite: `Please + base verb …` or `… , please.`

```text
Take a look at this ticket.

Go ahead.

Don't worry about it.

Please share your screen.

Send me the link, please.
```

> The base verb has no `-s`, `-ed`, or `-ing`: `take`, `check`, `be`. So it is `Be careful.`, not `Are careful.`

### 1.2 Meeting Imperatives Are Not Orders

The tone depends on what you add:

| Add | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `please` | more polite | `Please take a look.` |
| `just` | relaxed, "only a small thing" | `Just let me know.` |
| `feel free to` | an invitation, no pressure | `Feel free to jump in.` |
| context | the host runs the meeting | `Go ahead, Mia.` |

When a host says `Go ahead.`, it means "please start / you first", not "do it!".

### 1.3 High-Frequency Meeting Imperatives

| Sentence | Meaning | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Go ahead.` | you first, please speak | two people start talking; giving someone the floor |
| `Take a look.` | have a look | asking someone to check a doc, PR, or screen |
| `Let me know.` | tell me | asking for feedback |
| `Feel free to …` | you are welcome to … | inviting questions or additions |
| `Make sure …` | ensure … | reminders |
| `Hold on.` / `Give me a second.` | wait a moment | you are not ready yet |
| `Bear with me.` | please be patient | screen sharing, slow loading |
| `Keep me posted.` | update me | following someone else's task |
| `Never mind.` | forget it | taking back your question |

```text
Yeah, go ahead.

Feel free to jump in if I've missed anything.

Make sure the PR is linked to the ticket.

Bear with me, my screen is still loading.

Keep me posted on the pipeline issue.
```

### 1.4 Negative Imperatives: Don't …

**Pattern:** `Don't + base verb`

```text
Don't merge it before QA signs off.

Don't worry, it's not a blocker.

Please don't change the main branch directly.
```

## 2. Let's: Let's Do It Together ⭐

### 2.1 Running the Meeting

`Let's` = `Let us`, meaning "**we (you and I) will do this together**". Hosts say it almost every time they change topics.

**Pattern:** `Let's + base verb + rest.`

| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Let's start with …` / `Let's start from …` | begin with … |
| `Let's move to …` / `Let's move on to …` | continue with … |
| `Let's go to …` | go to … |
| `Let's go through …` | review … step by step |
| `Let's skip this one.` | skip it |
| `Let's go with …` | choose … |
| `Let's get started.` | start the meeting |
| `Let's take it offline.` | discuss it after the meeting |

```text
Somebody is already here, so let's get started.

Let's move to the native part.

Let's go through the open tickets one by one.

Most people voted five, so let's go with five.

That's a big topic. Let's take it offline.
```

### 2.2 Let's not … and shall we 🟢

The negative is `Let's not + base verb`. Some people say `Let's don't`, but it is not standard.

```text
Let's not spend too much time on this.

Let's take a five-minute break, shall we?
```

`…, shall we?` is British and rare in meetings. Recognize it only.

### 2.3 Let's say …: Suppose, For Example 🟡

`Let's say` means "**suppose / for example**". It is common in demos and design discussions.

```text
Let's say the user selects a new theme. Then the form looks like this.

Let's say we need three runners. How much will it cost?
```

## 3. Let me: I Will Do It

### 3.1 Announcing Your Own Action

`Let me + base verb` literally means "allow me to …", but in practice it means "**I'll do this now**". It announces your action politely.

```text
Let me share my screen.

Let me introduce the background first.

Let me double-check and get back to you.

Let me continue.

Let me show you the last one.
```

### 3.2 Let me see / Let me check: Thinking Aloud 🔴

When speakers need a moment to find information, they insert `let me see` or `let me check`, like "hmm, let me think". In subtitles, **skip it** and join the parts before and after.

```text
There are, let me check, four subtasks that need to continue in the next sprint.

The one highlight is about, let me see, the browser version support.
```

## 4. Let me know / Let us know: Tell Me

`Let me know` means **"tell me"**. It is one of the most common closing lines in meetings and emails.

**Pattern:** `Let me/us know + if clause / noun / wh-clause`

```text
Let me know if you have any questions.

Please take a look and let us know your feedback.

Let me know what you think.

Let me know if it's visible.
```

### 4.1 let's vs let us vs let me

| Form | Who | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `let's` | we, including the listener | let's do it together | `Let's move on.` |
| `let us` | we, not including the listener | allow us; tell us | `Let us know.` |
| `let me` | I | I'll do it | `Let me check.` |

`Let us know` is **not** shortened to `let's know`, because the listener is not part of "us".

### 4.2 let + person + base verb (no to)

The verb after `let` **never takes `to`**:

```text
Let us decide the final solution.

Let me check it.
```

❌ `let us to decide` ❌ `let me to check`

## 5. Polite Requests: From Direct to Very Polite

### 5.1 The Politeness Ladder

English adjusts politeness through **sentence patterns**. From top to bottom, the requests get more polite:

| Form | Politeness | Meeting frequency | When |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `Take a look.` | direct | 🔴 | close teammates, running the meeting |
| `Please take a look.` | polite | 🟡 | asking everyone to read a doc |
| `Can you take a look?` | polite, natural | 🔴 | the most common request |
| `Could you take a look?` | more polite | 🟡 | other teams, managers |
| `Could you please take a look?` | very polite | 🟡 | formal requests |
| `Would you mind taking a look?` | extra polite | 🟢 | asking for extra effort |
| `I was wondering if you could take a look.` | very indirect | 🟢 | when you are not sure they have time |

```text
Can you share the link in the chat?

Could you help us with the macOS runner setup?

Could you please approve the PR?

Would you mind sharing your screen?
```

`Can you …?` and `Could you …?` are **requests**, not questions about ability. `could` here is not past tense; it is just more polite. See [G11 Modal Verbs](11-modal-verbs.md).

### 5.2 Where to Put please

`please` can go at the start, at the end, or after `can/could you`:

```text
Please update the status in Jira.

Update the status in Jira, please.

Can you please update the status?
```

> ⚠️ Do not say `Please you update …`. Put the base verb right after `please`.
>
> `Please kindly …` is common in Indian English. It sounds over-polite to many native speakers. Recognize it; `Could you please …` is enough.

### 5.3 Answering Would you mind …?

`mind` means "object to". So:

- It takes `-ing`: `Would you mind checking …?`
- To **agree**, say "I don't mind": `No, not at all.` / `Sure, no problem.`
- Answering `Yes` actually means "I do mind".

```text
Would you mind moving the meeting to 3 PM?

No, not at all.
```

In casual speech many people simply answer `Sure.` to agree. It is understood.

### 5.4 Invitations: feel free to, go ahead, you can

These phrases **give the other person a chance**. They do not demand action:

```text
Feel free to drop off if you don't have any updates.

If anyone has a question, please go ahead.

Mia, you can go ahead.

Maybe you can check with the design team first.
```

Chinese colleagues often soften requests with `Maybe you can …`. It sounds natural and is worth copying.

### 5.5 Responding to Requests

| Response | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Sure.` / `Sure thing.` | okay |
| `No problem.` | no problem |
| `Will do.` | okay, I'll do it |
| `I'll take a look.` | I'll check it |
| `Let me check and get back to you.` | I'll confirm and reply |
| `Sorry, I can't today. Can I do it tomorrow?` | declining politely |

## 6. Exclamations: What a …! / How …! ⚪

> Almost never used in meetings. Read it once, or skip it.

Exclamations express strong feelings. Like imperatives, they are one of the four sentence types in school grammar, so they get a short note here.

**Patterns:**

- `What a/an + adjective + singular noun (+ subject + verb)!`
- `What + adjective + plural noun / uncountable noun!`
- `How + adjective / adverb (+ subject + verb)!`

```text
What a great demo!

What useful feedback!

How fast it is now!
```

In real meetings, people usually show the same feeling with short phrases:

```text
That's great!

Nice work, everyone.

Awesome, thanks for sharing.
```

## 7. Real Meeting Examples (Adapted)

These sentences come from real meeting subtitles. Recognition errors are fixed and names are anonymized.

```text

Since everyone has joined, let's get it started.

If there are no questions, let's move to the platform part.

Okay, that's all for the scrum updates, so let's move to the post-scrum discussion.

If you find that any of your stories doesn't fit any epic, please let us know.

If you need any help with this, Ken, please let me know.

For any technical foundation page updates, please just send the link to the Teams channel.

You can have a look and let us know if we need to dig further into the self-hosted solution.

Leo, could you also share the solution I gave you for the macOS runner setup?

Never mind, you can go ahead with the token topic.
```

Non-native original vs standard form:

| Subtitle original | Standard form |
| --- | --- |
| `Can let me share my screen?` | `Can I share my screen?` / `Let me share my screen.` |
| `… to let us to decide the final solution` | `… to let us decide the final solution` |
| `Please take a look on the list.` | `Please take a look at the list.` |
| `Let me know if any comments.` | `Let me know if you have any comments.` |
| `So, can you please approve that?` | correct — copy it |

## 8. Reading Imperatives in Subtitles

1. **Drop the opening fillers.** If a base verb follows `Okay, so, yeah, …`, it is an imperative or a `let's` sentence.
2. **After `let's`, read the verb.** That is the next step of the meeting: `let's move to …`, `let's go through …`.
3. **`let me` in the middle of a sentence** (with commas around it) is a thinking filler. Skip it.
4. **Questions starting with `can you / could you`** are almost always requests.
5. **`let me know` at the end** usually means "tell me if you have questions". The current part is ending.

Common speech-recognition (ASR) errors:

| Possibly seen in subtitles | Actually |
| --- | --- |
| `lets move to` | `let's move to` |
| `let me no` | `let me know` |
| `go head` | `go ahead` |
| `take a look of` | `take a look at` |
| `fill free to` | `feel free to` |

## 9. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Let's to start.` | `Let's start.` | base verb after `let's` |
| `Let me to check.` | `Let me check.` | no `to` after `let` |
| `Please you send me the link.` | `Please send me the link.` | no subject `you` |
| `Can you please to review it?` | `Can you please review it?` | base verb after `can` |
| `Would you mind to check it?` | `Would you mind checking it?` | `-ing` after `mind` |
| `Let's don't wait.` | `Let's not wait.` / `Don't wait.` | negative `let's not` |
| `Don't worried.` | `Don't worry.` | base verb after `don't` |
| `Let's discuss about it.` | `Let's discuss it.` | no `about` after `discuss` |
| `Let's know if …` | `Let us know if …` | "tell us" is not shortened |

## 10. Sentences You Can Use Today

```text
Let me share my screen. Can you see it?

Let's start with the web part.

Okay, let's move on to the next item.

Can you take a look at my PR when you have time?

Could you please share the link in the chat?

Let me check and get back to you.

Feel free to reach out if you need any help.

Please take a look and let me know if you have any comments.

Sorry, go ahead.
```
