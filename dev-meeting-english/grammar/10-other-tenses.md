## What This Lesson Solves

School grammar lists 16 tenses, but in real meetings **the five or six tenses from G07–G09 cover more than 99% of verbs**. The rest appear occasionally (recognize them) or almost never (skip them). Now and then you will see subtitles like these:

```text
I was working on the pipeline when the runner went down.

That job has been running for almost 15 minutes.
```

This lesson helps you **recognize** these less common tenses quickly, and gives you one table that shows which tenses to speak, which to recognize, and which to skip.

**After this lesson, you can:**

1. Recognize the past continuous and use the polite `I was wondering if …`.
2. Recognize the present perfect continuous and know it stresses "how long".
3. Understand the past perfect, future perfect, and future in the past when they show up.
4. Read the full 16-tense table and know which tenses meetings really need.

## 2. Past Continuous: was / were + -ing 🟢

### 2.1 Pattern

**Pattern:** `was / were + verb-ing`

```text
I was debugging the test when you called.

We were discussing this yesterday.
```

### 2.2 Use 1: In Progress at a Past Moment

```text
At 3 PM yesterday, I was still waiting for the build.

When I was working on the Harness pipeline, I found another issue.
```

### 2.3 Use 2: Background + Interruption

The longer action (past continuous) is the background; the short action (past simple) interrupts it:

```text
The pipeline was running when the runner went down.

While we were testing, QA found two more bugs.
```

`while` usually introduces the longer ongoing action; `when` usually introduces the short sudden one.

### 2.4 Use 3: Soft and Polite ⭐ Most Worth Learning

Using the past continuous for present thoughts softens them, like "I was just thinking …":

```text
I was wondering if there is any document about the new workflow.

I was thinking maybe we could use the same naming on both sides.

I was just checking the status with Ken.
```

`I was saying …` is often used to **repeat or clarify what you just said**:

```text
No, actually, I was saying that we're still open to other options.
```

## 3. Present Perfect Continuous: have / has been + -ing 🟢

### 3.1 Pattern and Meaning

**Pattern:** `have / has been + verb-ing`

Meaning: **started in the past and has continued until now** (it may still be going, or have just stopped). It stresses **the process and the duration**.

```text
That job has been running for almost 15 minutes.

I know I've been saying that for a while.

We've been waiting for access since last week.

I've been looking into this issue all morning.
```

### 3.2 Compared With the Present Perfect

| Present perfect | Present perfect continuous |
| --- | --- |
| result, amount | process, duration |
| `I've fixed three bugs.` | `I've been fixing bugs all day.` |
| `I've written the doc.` (finished) | `I've been writing the doc.` (working on it, maybe not done) |

```text
I've fixed three bugs today.

I've been fixing bugs all day.
```

> State verbs (`know`, `need`, `understand`) do not use this tense: say `I've known it for a while.`, not `I've been knowing`.

## 4. Past Perfect: had + Past Participle ⚪

> Almost never used in meetings. Read it once, or skip it.

**Pattern:** `had + past participle`. Meaning: "**the past before the past**". Of two past events, the earlier one takes the past perfect.

```text
I thought Ken had already reviewed it.

By the time we noticed, the job had failed twice.
```

**Subtitle note:** non-native speakers sometimes use `had` as a plain past tense, as in `I think Mia had reviewed the Figma file.` It just means `Mia reviewed / has reviewed it`.

`If we had known …` is a conditional; see [G21 Conditionals](21-conditionals.md).

## 5. Future Perfect: will have + Past Participle ⚪

> Almost never used in meetings. Read it once, or skip it.

**Pattern:** `will have + past participle`, often with `by + future time`: "will be completed before a future point".

```text
By Friday, we will have finished the migration.
```

In meetings, people usually say it more simply:

```text
We'll finish the migration by Friday.

The migration should be done by Friday.
```

## 6. Future in the Past: would / was going to ⚪

> Almost never used in meetings. Read it once, or skip it.

**Pattern:** `would + base verb` or `was / were going to + base verb`: "**the future, seen from a past moment**".

### 6.1 would: Mostly in Reported Speech

```text
Mia said she would send the doc today.
```

This belongs to reported speech; see [G22](22-reported-speech.md).

### 6.2 was going to: Planned, But Didn't Happen 🟢

This one is actually useful in meetings. It means "**I was about to / we had planned to**":

```text
I was going to ask the same question.

We were going to release it on Friday, but QA found a blocker.
```

## 7. The Full 16-Tense Table

An English tense = **4 times × 4 aspects**. With `he / work`, plus meeting frequency:

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | `works` 🔴 | `is working` 🔴 | `has worked` 🔴 | `has been working` 🟢 |
| **Past** | `worked` 🔴 | `was working` 🟢 | `had worked` ⚪ | `had been working` ⚪ |
| **Future** | `will work` 🔴 | `will be working` 🟢 | `will have worked` ⚪ | `will have been working` ⚪ |
| **Future in the past** | `would work` ⚪ | `would be working` ⚪ | `would have worked` ⚪ | `would have been working` ⚪ |

Each tense, with advice for meetings:

| # | Tense | Form | Freq. | Meeting example | Advice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | present simple | `do / does` | 🔴 | `It works on Android.` | speak it |
| 2 | present continuous | `am/is/are doing` | 🔴 | `I'm working on the API.` | speak it |
| 3 | present perfect | `have/has done` | 🔴 | `I've fixed the bug.` | speak it |
| 4 | present perfect continuous | `have/has been doing` | 🟢 | `It's been running for 15 minutes.` | recognize; speaking it is a bonus |
| 5 | past simple | `did` | 🔴 | `I fixed it yesterday.` | speak it |
| 6 | past continuous | `was/were doing` | 🟢 | `I was wondering if …` | recognize; speak the polite forms |
| 7 | past perfect | `had done` | ⚪ | `I thought he had reviewed it.` | recognize only |
| 8 | past perfect continuous | `had been doing` | ⚪ | `We had been waiting for a week.` | skip |
| 9 | simple future | `will do` / `be going to do` | 🔴 | `I'll take a look.` | speak it |
| 10 | future continuous | `will be doing` | 🟢 | `I'll be working on it next week.` | recognize only |
| 11 | future perfect | `will have done` | ⚪ | `We'll have finished it by Friday.` | skip |
| 12 | future perfect continuous | `will have been doing` | ⚪ | — | skip |
| 13 | future in the past | `would do` / `was going to do` | ⚪ | `I was going to ask that.` | recognize `was going to` |
| 14 | future-in-the-past continuous | `would be doing` | ⚪ | — | skip |
| 15 | future-in-the-past perfect | `would have done` | ⚪ | `It would have saved time.` (mostly conditionals) | see G21 |
| 16 | future-in-the-past perfect continuous | `would have been doing` | ⚪ | — | skip |

## 8. Stand-Up Tense Cheat Sheet

| What you say | First choice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| what you did yesterday | past simple | `Yesterday I fixed the login bug.` |
| what you have completed | present perfect | `I've raised a PR.` |
| what you're doing today | present continuous | `Today I'm working on the tests.` |
| what's next | `will` / `be going to` | `I'll start the migration next.` |
| blockers and status | present simple / continuous / perfect | `I'm blocked by the access issue.` / `I'm waiting for the API.` / `I still haven't got access.` |
| ongoing for a while | present perfect continuous | `I've been looking into it since Monday.` |

```text
Yesterday I fixed the login bug, and I've raised a PR. Today I'm working on the tests. I'm still waiting for the API, so I'll start the migration next.
```

## 10. Reading These Tenses in Subtitles

1. **`was / were + -ing`** → past continuous. If it starts with `I was wondering / I was thinking`, it is a soft question or suggestion about now.
2. **`been + -ing`** → perfect continuous. Look for `for / since` to see how long.
3. **`had + past participle`** → past perfect; but from non-native speakers, `had` is often just a plain past.
4. **Two meanings of `'d`:** `I'd` + past participle = `I had` (`I'd already left`); `I'd` + base verb = `I would` (`I'd suggest …`).
5. **`was going to`** → "had planned to", often followed by `but`.

## 11. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `I have been knowing it for a while.` | `I've known it for a while.` | state verb |
| `It is running for 15 minutes.` | `It has been running for 15 minutes.` | `for` until now → perfect continuous |
| `I am waiting since Monday.` | `I've been waiting since Monday.` | `since` → perfect (continuous) |
| `When I was work on it, …` | `When I was working on it, …` | past continuous needs `-ing` |
| `I had sent it yesterday.` | `I sent it yesterday.` | no earlier past event → past simple |
| `I was wonder if …` | `I was wondering if …` | past continuous needs `-ing` |
