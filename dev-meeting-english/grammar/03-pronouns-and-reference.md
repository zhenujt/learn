# G03 Pronouns and Reference

## What This Lesson Solves

In meetings, people rarely repeat full names. They say `it`, `this`, `that`, `they`, `this one`. Look at this subtitle:

```text
I checked it with them yesterday, and they said this one is fine, but that one still needs a fix.
```

You know every word. But what is `it`? Who are `them`? Which tickets are `this one` and `that one`? If you can't track them, you haven't really understood the sentence.

Think of a pronoun as a **pointer (reference)** in code: it has no content of its own. You must find what it points to.

**After this lesson, you can:**

1. Use the five forms of personal pronouns (`I / me / my / mine / myself`) and stop mixing up `he` and `she`.
2. Tell `it`, `this`, and `that` apart, and recognize the three roles of `that`.
3. Use `one / ones` to avoid repeating nouns: `the next one`, `this one`.
4. Quickly work out what `it / this / they` refers to in a subtitle.

## 1. Personal Pronouns: One Table, Five Forms 🔴

| Subject | Object | Possessive adjective | Possessive pronoun | Reflexive |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | me | my | mine | myself |
| you | you | your | yours | yourself / yourselves |
| he | him | his | his | himself |
| she | her | her | hers | herself |
| it | it | its | — | itself |
| we | us | our | ours | ourselves |
| they | them | their | theirs | themselves |

### 1.1 Subject vs Object Form: Use Position 🔴

- **Subject forms** go before the verb.
- **Object forms** go after a verb or a preposition.

```text
I will send it to you.

Can you help me with this?

Mia and I will work on it together.

Let's ask them.
```

In a compound subject, use `I` and put yourself last: `Mia and I`. `Let's` = `let us`.

### 1.2 he and she: The Classic Chinese-Speaker Mix-Up 🔴

In spoken Chinese, "he" and "she" sound the same, so many speakers mix them up in English. **Think about the person before you speak.** If you don't know or don't want to specify, use `they`:

```text
Ken is out today, so he will reply tomorrow.

Anna said she would review it.

If anyone has questions, they can ping me.
```

### 1.3 you, you guys, you all 🟡

`you` is both singular and plural. To make "all of you" clear, meetings use `you guys` or `you all`:

```text
Can you guys see my screen?

Thank you all for joining.
```

## 2. Possessives: my vs mine 🔴

**`my / your / our / their` need a noun after them; `mine / yours / ours / theirs` stand alone.**

```text
That's all for my part.

Nothing from my side.

Is this PR yours or mine?

Their team is working on the same feature.
```

> ⚠️ `its` = belonging to it; `it's` = `it is / it has`: `the page and its style`; `It's done.`

## 3. Reflexive Pronouns 🟢

Rare in meetings (about 1 per 10,000 words). Recognize a few fixed uses:

```text
I can do it by myself.

They don't depend on each other.

The page itself is fine, but the API is slow.
```

## 4. it: The Busiest Pronoun in Meetings 🔴

### 4.1 Referring to a Thing or a Whole Situation

```text
I found a bug in the login page, and I'm fixing it now.

We discussed the timeline. It's still not final.

I'll look into it.
```

### 4.2 Dummy Subject: It's + Adjective + to do / that … 🔴

English avoids long subjects at the start. It puts `it` first as a placeholder and moves the real subject to the end:

```text
It's hard to say right now.

It's good to know.

It's not necessary to change the API.

It looks like the build is broken.

It seems that nobody has checked it yet.
```

Here `it` **points to nothing**; don't look for a referent.

### 4.3 Time, Situation, Weather

```text
It's 9 PM here.

It's fine. Don't worry.

It's been a busy week.
```

### 4.4 Fixed Phrases

```text
That's it.

Go for it.

Let's leave it for now.

I'll make it.
```

## 5. this / that / these / those 🔴

**The core difference is distance.** In meetings, distance is often **time and the shared screen**:

| Word | Usually refers to | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `this` | something on the shared screen; what I'm about to say | `This is the PR I mentioned.` |
| `that` | what the other person just said; something earlier | `That makes sense.` |
| `these` | several things on screen | `These two tickets are related.` |
| `those` | several things mentioned earlier | `Those issues are fixed now.` |

```text
This is the new dashboard.

That's a good point.

That's right.

That makes sense to me.

These are the open tickets for this sprint.

I think those comments are already resolved.
```

**When responding, use `that`:** `That sounds good.`, `That's fine.`, `That's all.` Here `that` = "what you just said".

### 5.1 The Three Roles of that 🔴

`that` is everywhere in subtitles, but it does not always mean "that thing":

| Role | How to recognize it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demonstrative pronoun | stands alone as subject or object | `That's fine.` |
| Conjunction (can be dropped) | after `think / know / said / make sure` | `I think that it works.` (see [G18](18-noun-clauses.md)) |
| Relative pronoun | after a noun | `the bug that we found` (see [G19](19-relative-clauses.md)) |

```text
That is the main problem.

Ken said that the fix is ready.

This is the ticket that I created yesterday.
```

## 6. one / ones: Avoid Repeating Nouns 🔴

`one` replaces a **countable noun** already mentioned; the plural is `ones`. Hosts say it constantly when going through tickets:

```text
Let's move to the next one.

Do we need to vote for this one?

Which one is better?

The old tests are fine, but the new ones are failing.

Not this one, sorry. The one below.
```

Two notes:

- `one` cannot replace an uncountable noun: use `the feedback`, not "a feedback one".
- In `one of the + plural`, `one` is a number: `One of the tests is failing.`

## 7. Indefinite Pronouns: someone, anything, nothing… 🔴

| | -one / -body (people) | -thing (things) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| some- | `someone` | `something` |
| any- | `anyone` (questions, negatives) | `anything` |
| no- | `no one` / `nobody` | `nothing` |
| every- | `everyone` | `everything` |

**Rule: as a subject, they take a singular verb.**

```text
Does anyone have any questions?

Everyone is here, so let's get started.

Is there anything else?

Nothing from my side.

Something is wrong with the pipeline.

Can someone take this ticket?
```

Adjectives come **after** them: `something new`, `anything else`, `nothing special`.

### 7.1 other, another, the other, others 🟡

| Phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `another` | one more (of many) | `Let's try another option.` |
| `the other` | the second of two | `One is for iOS; the other is for Android.` |
| `other + plural` | different ones | `other teams` |
| `others` | other people or things | `I'll check with others.` |
| `the others` | all the rest | `The others are done.` |
| `each other` | mutually | `They depend on each other.` |

## 8. Tracking References in Subtitles

To find what `it / this / they` points to, try these four rules in order:

1. **Find the nearest noun that matches in number.** `it` → singular; `they` → plural or a team.
2. **During screen sharing, `this` and `here` usually mean something on the screen.**
3. **`it / this / that` often mean "the whole thing just mentioned"**, not one specific word.
4. **`they` often means another team, company, or group of people**, who may not be named in that sentence.

> Subtitle: We noticed that you have reached out to the tool's dev team, and they provided a reference doc.

`they` = `the tool's dev team` (the nearest noun for a group of people).

> Subtitle: All of them are blocked by the approval. If you ask the cloud engineer to set up the resources, they will ask you for the approved document.

The first `them` = the tasks mentioned before; the second `they` = `the cloud engineer` (meaning cloud engineers in general, so `they`).

## 9. Real Meeting Examples (Adapted)

```text
Ken is taking it, but I don't think it's started yet.

These two tasks depend on this one.

I see that Anna approved it, but I can't edit it yet.

If anyone hasn't read it, they can read through the page.

Okay, let's have a second vote for this one.

So that's something we need to figure out with the native team.

Except this one, does anyone have other topics to discuss?
```

## 10. Reading Subtitles

1. **`this this this` / `it, it's, it's not` are stutters and restarts; keep the last version.**
2. **he/she mix-ups are common.** Non-native speakers often get them wrong; trust the name.
3. **`you mean …?` is usually followed by what the other person just said**, to confirm understanding; see [G05](05-questions-and-negatives.md).
4. **Recognize, but don't copy, non-native forms:**

| Seen in subtitles | Standard form |
| --- | --- |
| `Ken … she will update it` (Ken is a man) | `he will update it` |
| `Does anyone else has questions?` | `Does anyone else have questions?` |
| `in my side` | `on my side` / `from my side` |
| `I and Mia will do it.` | `Mia and I will do it.` |
| `Me also.` | `Me too.` |
| `This PR, it is ready.` | `This PR is ready.` (fine in speech, not in writing) |

## 11. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Ken said she will fix it.` | `Ken said he will fix it.` | `he` for men, `she` for women |
| `Me and Mia will do it.` | `Mia and I will do it.` | subject form; yourself last |
| `Send it to I.` | `Send it to me.` | object form after a preposition |
| `Does anyone has questions?` | `Does anyone have questions?` | base verb after `does` |
| `Everyone are here.` | `Everyone is here.` | indefinite pronouns are singular |
| `It's status is green.` | `Its status is green.` | `its` = belonging to it |
| `Is very hard to say.` | `It's very hard to say.` | dummy subject `it` is required |
| `I need else something.` | `I need something else.` | modifiers go after indefinite pronouns |
| `This is mine PR.` | `This is my PR.` / `This PR is mine.` | `mine` cannot go before a noun |

## 12. Sentences You Can Use Today

```text
That makes sense. Thanks.

I'll look into it after the meeting.

It's hard to say right now. I'll update you tomorrow.

Can someone take this one?

This is the PR I mentioned yesterday.

Is there anything else?

Nothing from my side.

Let's move to the next one.
```

## 13. Practice

**A. What does each bold pronoun refer to?**

1. I created a ticket for the login bug, and Kevin will pick **it** up.
2. We asked the security team, and **they** said we need an approval.
3. There are two options. **The first one** is faster, but **the other** is cheaper.
4. Okay, this is the new dashboard. **These** are the charts for last week.
5. — We can skip the demo today. — **That**'s fine with me.

**B. Fill in the pronoun**

1. Can you send the link to ___ (the speaker)?
2. Lily is on leave, so ___ will reply next week.
3. ___ hard to say when it will be ready.
4. Does ___ (some person, in a question) have any questions?
5. The new tests pass, but the old ___ fail.
6. Emma and ___ (the speaker) will do the demo.

**C. Correct the sentences**

1. Everyone have the access now.
2. Is not necessary to update the doc.
3. Ben is busy today, so she can't join.
4. Its already merged.
