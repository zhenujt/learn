## What This Lesson Solves

Whenever someone starts to present, the first sentence is almost always:

```text
Let me share my screen. Can you see my screen?
```

These "process sentences" appear constantly in meetings, but few people study them. As a result, when someone asks `Is my screen visible?` you don't know whether to answer; when it's your turn to demo, you don't know how to begin; when the host says `Let's take it offline.` you keep talking.

There are not many of these sentences, and they are very fixed. **Once you know them, you sound calm and in control.** One day you will host a meeting or give a demo, so this lesson prepares you for that too.

**After this lesson, you can:**

1. Open a meeting, give the agenda, call on people, and hand over with fixed sentences.
2. Share your screen and confirm people can see it; handle small problems such as a black screen or bad audio.
3. Invite questions, switch topics, and move a long discussion to after the meeting.
4. Recognize the host's process signals and know where the meeting is.

## 1. The Meeting Moment

The host (often the Scrum Master) does these things in a meeting:

| Step | What the host does | Typical sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | Greet, wait for people | `Hi, good morning, good evening, everyone.` `Let's wait one more minute.` |
| Agenda | Say what today's parts are | `Today we have three parts.` |
| Share | Open the board or a document | `Let me share my screen. Is my screen visible?` |
| Call on / hand over | Ask someone to speak | `Ken, you can go ahead.` |
| Invite questions | After each part | `Any questions or comments?` |
| Switch | Go to the next part | `If no questions, let's move on.` |
| Keep time | Stop long discussions | `Let's take this offline.` |
| Close | End the meeting (see M07) | `That's all for today. Thank you.` |

Your meetings cross time zones (China, India, the UK, the US), so the greeting is often a chain: `Good morning, good afternoon, good evening, everyone.` It is not a mistake; it **includes everyone in every time zone**.

## 2. The Speaking Formula

**Six steps to host a part of the meeting:**

> ① Open `Hi, everyone. Let's get started.` → ② Agenda `Today we have …` → ③ Share and check `Let me share my screen. Can you see it?` → ④ Present or call on `For …, …` / `Ken, you can go ahead.` → ⑤ Questions `Any questions or comments?` → ⑥ Switch `If no questions, let's move on to …`

**When it's your turn to demo or present:**

> `Let me share my screen.` → `Can you see my screen?` → `Today I will show you …` → demo → `That's it for the demo. Any questions?` → `Let me stop sharing.`

## 3. Sentences by Function

### 3.1 Opening and Waiting

```text
Hi, good morning, good afternoon, good evening, everyone.

Let's wait one more minute for others to join.

I think most people are here, so let's get started.

I see Ken hasn't joined yet, so let's start with the native part first.
```

### 3.2 Giving the Agenda

```text
Today we have three parts.

First, the high-level updates, then the story updates, and at the end, the post-scrum discussion.

We have two demos today. Priya, you can do the first one.

Does anyone have any topic to bring up today?
```

### 3.3 Sharing the Screen ⭐ Most Frequent

```text
Let me share my screen.

Can you see my screen?

Is my screen visible now?

Yes, I can see your screen.

Not yet. It's still loading.

I think you are sharing the wrong window.

Could you zoom in a little? The text is too small.

Okay, let me stop sharing. Ken, you can share yours.
```

When someone asks `Can you see my screen?`, **someone must answer**. If another person already said `Yes`, you don't need to repeat it. If nobody speaks, say `Yes, we can see it.` It is an easy first step to speaking up.

### 3.4 Audio and Network Problems 🟡

```text
Can you hear me?

Sorry, I was on mute.

I think you're on mute.

Sorry, you're breaking up a little. Could you say that again?

Sorry, my network is not stable today.
```

### 3.5 Calling On People and Handing Over

```text
Ken, you can go ahead.

Priya, do you want to start?

Okay, over to you, Leo.

Nina, I saw you raised your hand. Please go ahead.
```

`go ahead` has three uses in meetings: ① "you can speak now"; ② "please continue" (after an interruption); ③ "do it" (agreeing to someone's proposal). Use the context to decide.

### 3.6 Inviting Questions

```text
Any questions or comments?

Does anyone have any questions regarding this part?

If you have any questions during the demo, feel free to interrupt me.

You can also leave your questions in the chat.
```

### 3.7 Switching Topics

```text
If no questions, let's move on to the next topic.

Okay, let's move to the platform part.

That's all for the scrum updates. Let's move to the post-scrum discussion.
```

### 3.8 Keeping Time and Moving Discussions Offline

```text
We are running out of time, so let's take this offline.

Let's not go too deep into the details in this meeting.

Let's put this in the parking lot and come back to it later.

We have five minutes left.
```

`take it offline` does not mean "disconnect". It means **"discuss it after the meeting, with only the people involved"**. A `parking lot` is a list of off-topic items to handle later.

### 3.9 Giving a Demo

```text
Today I will do a quick demo of the token sync workflow.

As you can see here, the button now supports dark mode.

Let me go back to the previous page.

That's it for the demo. Any questions?
```

## 4. Full Dialogue: Alex Hosts the Post-Scrum Session for the First Time (Adapted from Real Meetings)

Scene: after the scrum updates, Grace asks Alex to host the post-scrum discussion. There is one demo today.

```text
Grace: That's all for the scrum updates. Alex, can you host the post-scrum session today?

Alex: Sure. So, does anyone have any topic to bring up today?

Priya: Yes, I can do a quick demo of the PR automation.

Alex: Great. Priya, please go ahead.

Priya: Thanks. Let me share my screen. Is my screen visible?

Alex: Not yet. I can only see a black screen.

Priya: Sorry, let me share it again. How about now?

Alex: Yes, now we can see it.

Priya: Okay. As you can see here, when someone requests a review on GitHub, a message is sent to our Teams channel automatically.

Ken: Sorry to interrupt. Does it also work for draft PRs?

Priya: Not yet. That's a good point. I will check it later.

Priya: That's it for the demo. Any questions?

Ken: Looks good. But I think we need to discuss the permission part in more detail.

Alex: Since we are running out of time, let's take the permission part offline. Ken and Priya, can you set up a quick call?

Ken: Sure.

Alex: Thanks, Priya, for the demo. Any other topics?

Alex: Okay, if no other topics, that's all for today. Thank you, everyone.
```

## 5. When You Hear This…

| You hear | It means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Is my screen visible?` / `Can you see my screen?` | The presenter is checking the share | If nobody answers, say `Yes, we can see it.` |
| `Let's get started.` | The meeting officially begins | Close other windows, turn on subtitles |
| `Any questions or comments?` | A part has ended; questions are welcome | Ask now, or stay quiet |
| `Please go ahead.` / `Over to you.` | The named person should speak | If it's you, start right away |
| `Feel free to interrupt.` | Interruptions are welcome | Use `Sorry to interrupt, …` to ask |
| `Let's move on.` / `Let's move to …` | Next part | Check whether it's your module |
| `Let's take this offline.` | Discuss it after the meeting | Stop the discussion, follow up later |
| `I have a hard stop at 8.` | They must leave at 8 | Ask them your question soon |

## 6. Reading the Subtitles

| You may see | It means / Standard form |
| --- | --- |
| `is my ice cream visible` | `is my screen visible` |
| `I'm my screen baseball` | `is my screen visible` |
| `could you see my screen right now` | `can you see my screen now` (same meaning) |
| `post-scram session` | `post-scrum session` |
| `if no, let's move on` | `if not, let's move on` / `if there are no questions, let's move on` |
| `let's discuss about it offline` | `let's discuss it offline` |

**How to read:** process sentences are short and fixed. **You can guess the whole sentence from the first few words.** `Let me sh…` is always "share my screen"; `Any que…` is always "any questions". Save your attention for the content sentences.

## 7. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `I share screen to you.` | `Let me share my screen.` | `share my screen` is fixed; no `to you` |
| `Everybody can see?` | `Can everyone see it?` | Questions put `can` first |
| `Please open your mic.` | `Please unmute yourself.` | English uses `unmute` |
| `Open your camera.` | `Turn on your camera.` | Devices use `turn on / turn off` |
| `Let's move to next topic.` | `Let's move to the next topic.` | `next topic` needs `the` |
| `If no question, …` | `If there are no questions, …` | The full sentence is clearer |
| `Let's discuss about it later.` | `Let's discuss it later.` | `discuss` takes no `about` |

## 8. Your Hosting Template

| Step | Template | Hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | `Hi, everyone. I think most people are here, so let's get started.` | Greet and start |
| Agenda | `Today we have ________ parts. First, ________. Then, ________.` | Give the agenda |
| Share | `Let me share my screen. Can you see it?` | Share and check |
| Call on | `________, you can go ahead.` | Ask someone to speak |
| Questions | `Any questions or comments on ________?` | After each part |
| Switch | `If no questions, let's move on to ________.` | Next part |
| Time | `We are running out of time, so let's take ________ offline.` | Move it after the meeting |

The demo version:

```text
Let me share my screen. Can you see it? Today I will do a quick demo of the new date picker. As you can see here, you can now select a date range. That's it for the demo. Any questions? Okay, let me stop sharing.
```

## 10. Practice

**A. Choose the best response**

1. Someone asks: `Is my screen visible?` (You can see it.)
2. Someone asks: `Can you hear me?` (The audio keeps cutting out.)
3. The host says: `Alex, you can go ahead.`
4. You are the host. The discussion keeps growing and there are 3 minutes left.

a. `Sure. A quick update from my side.` b. `Yes, we can see it.` c. `We are running out of time, so let's take this offline.` d. `Sorry, you're breaking up a little.`

**B. Correct the sentences**

1. Everybody can see my screen?
2. Let's move to next part.
3. Please open your mic, Ken.
