# M07 Wrap-Up, Action Items, and Follow-Up

## What This Lesson Solves

After twenty minutes of discussion, everyone says `Okay, sounds good. Bye.` and leaves. The next day you find out that nobody knows who is actually doing the work.

The value of a meeting is in the last minute: **what was decided, who does it, and by when**. A clear wrap-up sounds like this:

```text
So, to summarize, Alex will update the ticket, and Priya will check the license by Friday.
```

**After this lesson, you can:**

1. Summarize the conclusion of a topic with `So, to summarize, …`
2. State and confirm action items as "who + what + when".
3. Volunteer for a task and make clear follow-up promises.
4. End a meeting with fixed sentences, say goodbye across time zones, and send a short summary message afterwards.

## 1. The Meeting Moment: Two Kinds of Wrap-Up

| Type | When | Typical sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Topic wrap-up | When a discussion ends | `So, we can take this as a follow-up item.` |
| Meeting wrap-up | At the end of the whole meeting | `Any other topics? … Okay, that's all for today.` |
| Follow-up | After the meeting | `I'll update the ticket after the meeting.` |

In your real meetings, the meeting wrap-up follows the same order almost every time:

1. The host asks: `Any other topics?` / `Anyone have any other questions?`
2. After a short silence: `It seems no, so that's all for today.`
3. Someone says: `We can end the call here.` / `We can drop the call then.`
4. Everyone thanks and says goodbye: `Thank you, everyone. Have a good day. Bye-bye.`

Because the meetings cross time zones, goodbyes do too: say `Have a good day.` to UK and US colleagues, and `Have a good night.` or `Good night.` to colleagues in China and India.

**The topic wrap-up is the one people skip.** In your meetings, `action item` appears less than once per 10,000 words and `follow up` about 4 times; many discussions just end with `we can discuss offline`. So **if the task involves you, make sure you know who is doing what**.

## 2. The Speaking Formula

**Action item = who + what + when**

> `Alex` + `will update the ticket` + `by Friday.`

If any part is missing, it isn't clear. Without "who", nobody does it; without "when", it drags on.

**Closing a topic:**

> `So, to summarize, + conclusion.` → `+ who + will + action + time.` → `Is that correct?` / `Does that work for everyone?`

**Closing the meeting (host):**

> `Any other topics?` → `If not, that's all for today.` → `Thank you, everyone. Have a good day.`

## 3. Sentences by Function

### 3.1 Summarizing a Topic

```text
So, to summarize, we will keep the local runner for now.

Okay, so the conclusion is that we go with the self-hosted website.

So we agree to put this story on hold until the approval is done.

Let me quickly recap the action items.
```

### 3.2 Confirming Who Does It ⭐ Most Important

```text
Who will take this?

Alex, can you take this as a follow-up item?

Sure, I'll take it.

Ken and Priya will follow up on this.

I'll leave it to you to decide who picks it up.

Let's create a follow-up story to track it.
```

When you are named, **accept directly** with `Sure, I'll take it.` If you can't, give the reason: `I can take it, but not until next week, because I'm on the release this week.`

### 3.3 Making the Time Clear

```text
I'll update the ticket by end of day.

Can you share it before the next sync?

Let's check it again in tomorrow's daily sync.

We will have a follow-up meeting next week.
```

`by Friday` means **"no later than Friday"**; `on Friday` means "on that day". Action items almost always use `by`. See [G16](../grammar/16-prepositions.md).

### 3.4 Moving Discussions After the Meeting

```text
Let's discuss this offline.

I'll set up a quick call with Ken tomorrow.

Let's continue the discussion in the channel.

I'll align with the design team offline and update everyone.
```

### 3.5 What You Will Do After the Meeting (Promises)

```text
I'll send the link in the chat.

I'll update the ticket after the meeting.

I'll take a look and get back to you if I have any questions.

I'll share the document in the engineering channel today.
```

`I'll …` here is a **promise**. Once you say it, do it. If you're not sure, say `I'll try to …` or give a later time.

### 3.6 Ending the Meeting

```text
Any other topics?

It seems no, so that's all for today.

Okay, we can end the call here.

Thanks for joining, and thank you for your time.
```

### 3.7 Goodbyes Across Time Zones

```text
Thank you, everyone. Have a good day.

Have a good night. Bye.

See you tomorrow.

Have a nice weekend.
```

### 3.8 The Follow-Up Message (Written in Teams) 🟡

A short written summary after the meeting is the most effective follow-up. Writing is easier than speaking, **so it suits you while your spoken English is still growing**.

```text
Hi all, here is a quick summary of today's discussion.

1. We will keep the local runner as a short-term solution.

2. Alex and Priya will research the cloud runner option by Friday.

3. Ken will check the license status with the platform team.

Please let me know if I missed anything.
```

## 4. Full Dialogue: Closing the Post-Scrum Discussion (Adapted from Real Meetings)

Scene: the post-scrum discussion is nearly over. The team has just discussed automating PR review notifications. Grace is hosting, Ken is the tech lead, and Priya and Alex are developers.

```text
Grace: Okay, I think we have discussed a lot. So, to summarize, we want to send a Teams message automatically when someone requests a PR review.

Ken: Yes. And we need to check if the automation needs a premium license.

Grace: Right. So, who can take this?

Alex: I can take the research part. I'll check the license and the existing documents.

Grace: Great. When do you think you can share the result?

Alex: I think I can share it by Thursday, before the next sync.

Ken: Sounds good. I have a document from the platform team about sending notifications to Teams. I'll share the link with you after the meeting.

Alex: Thanks, Ken. That will help a lot.

Grace: Okay, so let me quickly recap. Alex will research the license and share the result by Thursday. Ken will send the document link today. Is that correct?

Alex: Yes, that's correct.

Grace: Perfect. Any other topics?

Priya: Nothing from my side.

Grace: Okay, it seems no, so that's all for today. Thank you, everyone.

Ken: Thanks, guys. Have a good day. Bye.

Alex: Thank you. Have a good night. Bye-bye.
```

Notice the three things Alex does well: **volunteers** (`I can take the research part.`), **gives a time** (`by Thursday`), and **confirms the summary** (`Yes, that's correct.`).

## 5. When You Hear This…

| You hear | It means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `So, to summarize, …` / `Let me recap.` | The host is stating the conclusion | Read carefully; this is the key information |
| `Who will take this?` / `Can someone pick this up?` | Looking for an owner | If you want it, say `I can take it.` |
| `Alex, can you take this as a follow-up item?` | The task is assigned to you | `Sure, I'll take it.` and give a time |
| `I'll leave it to you.` | You (as a group) decide | Agree with your colleagues after the meeting |
| `Let's discuss offline.` / `Let's set up a call.` | Talk separately later | Offer a time |
| `Any other topics?` | The meeting is about to end | Speak now, or stay quiet |
| `We can end the call here.` / `We can drop the call.` | The meeting is over | Thank and say goodbye |
| `I'll get back to you.` | They will reply later | Note it; follow up if they don't |

## 6. Reading the Subtitles

| You may see | It means / Standard form |
| --- | --- |
| `we can allow with Mia offline` | `we can align with Mia offline` |
| `I'll see you in the deleting cup meeting` | `I'll see you in the daily sync-up meeting` |
| `discuss it in the post graph` | `discuss it in the post-scrum session` |
| `Your eyes, Bay.` / `Bagde.` | `Bye, guys.` / `Bye.` (goodbye noise; ignore it) |
| `we think we can end today's sync up meeting` | `I think we can end today's sync-up meeting` |
| `I will follow up this` | `I will follow up on this` |

**How to read:** in the last two minutes of subtitles, **`will` and people's names** matter most. Treat every `X will …` as an action item and copy it into your notes.

## 7. Common Mistakes

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `I will follow up this.` | `I will follow up on this.` | `follow up on + thing`, `follow up with + person` |
| `Let's discuss it in offline.` | `Let's discuss it offline.` | `offline` is an adverb; no `in` |
| `I will feedback to you.` | `I'll get back to you.` / `I'll give you feedback.` | `feedback` is a noun |
| `See you tomorrow's meeting.` | `See you in tomorrow's meeting.` | Needs `in` |
| `I will send you after meeting.` | `I'll send it to you after the meeting.` | Needs the object `it` and `the` |
| `Who will take charge this?` | `Who will take charge of this?` / `Who will take this?` | `take charge of` |
| `Have a nice weekends.` | `Have a nice weekend.` | Singular after `a` |

## 8. Your Wrap-Up Template

**When a task is given to you:**

| Step | Template | Hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accept | `Sure, I'll take it.` / `I can take the ________ part.` | Take the task |
| Time | `I think I can ________ by ________.` | Give a time |
| Confirm | `So, just to confirm, I will ________ by ________. Right?` | Restate it |

**When you host the wrap-up:**

| Step | Template | Hint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Summarize | `So, to summarize, ________.` | Conclusion |
| Action items | `________ will ________ by ________.` | Who + what + when |
| Confirm | `Is that correct? Does that work for everyone?` | Let people confirm |
| End | `Any other topics? … If not, that's all for today. Thank you, everyone.` | Close the meeting |

A filled-in example:

```text
So, to summarize, we will move the theme story to the next sprint. Alex will update the ticket by end of day, and Priya will check the design with the design team by Friday. Is that correct? Okay, any other topics? If not, that's all for today. Thank you, everyone. Have a good day.
```

**B. Correct the sentences**

1. I will follow up this issue.

3. I will feedback to you tomorrow.
4. See you tomorrow's meeting.

Content: today the team decided to use the local runner for now; Alex will research the cloud option by Friday; Ken will check the license; ask people to tell you if anything is missing.
