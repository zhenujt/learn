## How to Use These Tests

Take each test as soon as you finish its stage. Don't wait until the end of the course.

| Test | Covers | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stage 1 | G01–G06: sentence skeleton, nouns and articles, pronouns, be and there be, questions and negatives, imperatives | after G06 |
| Stage 2 | G07–G10: tenses | after G10 |
| Stage 3 | G11–G17: modals, passive, non-finite verbs, phrasal verbs, adjectives and adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions | after G17 |
| Stage 4 | G18–G24: clauses, conditionals, reported speech, spoken grammar, advanced grammar | after G24 |
| Final test | M01–M07: real meeting usage | after M07 |

## Scoring Guide

- **5 points per question**, 20 questions, 100 points total.
- A question with two blanks gives 2.5 points per blank.
- Translation and situation questions: correct meaning and grammar = 5 points; correct meaning with a small grammar slip (article, plural) = 3 points; wrong meaning = 0. Your answer does not have to match the key word for word.
- **70 points = pass**; move on to the next stage. **90 or more** means you have mastered the stage.
- **If you don't pass:** every answer shows its lesson. Reread those lessons, especially "Common Mistakes" and "Practice", and retake the test two or three days later. Lesson lists: [Grammar index](../grammar/README.md) and [Meetings index](../meetings/README.md).
- Even if you pass, review any lesson where you missed two or more questions.

## Stage 1: G01–G06

**A. Find the subject and the main verb**

1. For the web part, most stories are completed.
2. The PR that Ken opened yesterday needs one more approval.

**B. Name the sentence pattern** (SV / SVC / SVO / SVOO / SVOC)

3. We need more time.
4. This change makes the test unstable.

**C. Articles, nouns, and quantities**

5. I found ___ issue in ___ login API.
6. We still have two ___ (subtask) left.
7. How ___ time do we need? (much / many)
8. Correct it: We need more informations about the bug.

**D. Pronouns**

9. Ken and Mia finished the API. ___ will demo it tomorrow.
10. Is this the latest build, or is there a newer ___? (one / it)
11. Correct it: Me and Ken will check it.

**E. be and there be**

12. ___ two options for the runner.
13. Correct it: There have some issues in the pipeline.
14. Ken ___ out of office yesterday.

**F. Questions, negatives, and short answers**

15. Make it a question: The build is green.
16. Make it negative: We need a new ticket.
17. Did you merge the PR? — Yes, I ___.

**G. Imperatives and Let's**

18. Which is better for asking a colleague in a meeting? A. Give me the link. B. Could you send me the link, please?
19. ___ move to the next topic.
20. Correct it: Let's to check the logs first.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Subject `most stories`, verb `are completed` (`For the web part` is the topic) → G01
2. Subject `The PR`, verb `needs` (`that Ken opened yesterday` is a modifier) → G01
3. SVO → G01
4. SVOC (`the test` = object, `unstable` = complement) → G01
5. an, the → G02
6. subtasks → G02
7. much (`time` is uncountable) → G02
8. We need more information about the bug. (`information` is uncountable) → G02
9. They → G03
10. one (another of the same kind) → G03
11. Ken and I will check it. → G03
12. There are → G04
13. There are some issues in the pipeline. → G04
14. was → G04
15. Is the build green? → G05
16. We don't need a new ticket. → G05
17. did → G05
18. B → G06
19. Let's → G06
20. Let's check the logs first. (base verb after `let's`) → G06

</details>

## Stage 2: G07–G10

**A. Use the correct form of the verb**

1. I ___ (work) on the login bug right now.
2. The pipeline usually ___ (take) about 20 minutes.
3. She ___ (join) the team last month.
4. I ___ already ___ (fix) the issue.
5. ___ you ___ (test) it on iOS yet?
6. Yesterday I ___ (talk) to the PM, and she ___ (say) it's fine.
7. We ___ (release) on Thursday. It's already on the calendar.

**B. Choose**

8. Ken ___ out of office since Monday. (was / has been)
9. A: The build just failed. B: Okay, I ___ check it now. (will / am going to) (a decision made on the spot)
10. Which sentence describes a fixed arrangement? A. I'll do it. B. I'm meeting the PM tomorrow.

**C. Correct the sentences**

11. I am knowing the root cause.
12. I have finished it yesterday.
13. I will to send it tomorrow.
14. He don't work on Fridays.
15. When it will be ready, I'll let you know.

**D. Say it in English**

16. Tell the team you are currently waiting for feedback from the design team.
17. Tell the team you have submitted the PR (no time given).
18. Promise to update the ticket tomorrow.

**E. Tenses in subtitles: name the tense and explain the meaning**

19. We were working on it when the server went down.
20. By Friday, we will have finished the migration.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. am working → G07
2. takes → G07
3. joined → G08
4. have, fixed → G08
5. Have, tested → G08
6. talked, said → G08
7. are releasing (present continuous for an arrangement) → G09
8. has been (`since` + starting point) → G08
9. will (decision on the spot) → G09
10. B → G09
11. I know the root cause. (`know` is a state verb) → G07
12. I finished it yesterday. (`yesterday` needs the past simple) → G08
13. I will send it tomorrow. → G09
14. He doesn't work on Fridays. → G07
15. When it's ready, I'll let you know. (present tense in a time clause) → G09
16. We're waiting for feedback from the design team. → G07
17. I've submitted the PR. → G08
18. I'll update the ticket tomorrow. → G09
19. Past continuous: we were in the middle of the work when the server failed. → G10
20. Future perfect: the migration will be complete no later than Friday. (🟢 recognize only) → G10

</details>

## Stage 3: G11–G17

**A. Modal verbs**

1. We ___ finish it today; the release is tomorrow. (need to / might)
2. ___ you share your screen, please?
3. It ___ be a cache issue, but I'm not sure.
4. Correct it: We should to check it first.

**B. Passive voice**

5. The PR ___ (approve) yesterday.
6. The bug ___ (fix) in the next release.
7. Correct it: The ticket was create by Mia.

**C. to do / doing / done**

8. We need ___ (update) the docs.
9. Thanks for ___ (share) the link.
10. I'm looking forward to ___ (hear) your feedback.

**D. Phrasal verbs and collocations**

11. Can you ___ ___ this issue? (investigate)
12. We need to ___ a decision this week. (make / do)

**E. Adjectives, adverbs, and comparison**

13. The new runner is much ___ (fast) than the old one.
14. All the tests ran ___ (successful).

**F. Prepositions**

15. The story is ready ___ review.
16. Please finish it ___ Friday. (no later than)
17. Correct it: We need to discuss about the timeline.

**G. Conjunctions**

18. The build failed, ___ we couldn't deploy.
19. Correct it: Although it's done, but we need more tests.
20. Join with `because`: We postponed the release. QA found a bug.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. need to → G11
2. Could (or Can) → G11
3. might / may / could → G11
4. We should check it first. → G11
5. was approved → G12
6. will be fixed → G12
7. The ticket was created by Mia. → G12
8. to update → G13
9. sharing → G13
10. hearing (`to` in `look forward to` is a preposition) → G13
11. look into → G14
12. make → G14
13. faster → G15
14. successfully → G15
15. for → G16
16. by → G16
17. We need to discuss the timeline. → G16
18. so → G17
19. Although it's done, we need more tests. (or `It's done, but we need more tests.`) → G17
20. We postponed the release because QA found a bug. → G17

</details>

## Stage 4: G18–G24

**A. Noun clauses**

1. Correct it: I'm not sure what is the problem.
2. Can you check ___ the build passed? (whether)
3. Say it in English: give your opinion that the team can leave the item for now.

**B. Relative clauses**

4. This is the bug ___ Ken reported last week.
5. We need someone ___ knows Android well.
6. This is the page ___ you can find all the docs.

**C. Adverbial clauses**

7. ___ the PR is approved, we'll merge it. (as soon as)
8. Correct it: Once it will be ready, I'll tell you.
9. We can't release ___ QA signs off. (not before)

**D. Conditionals**

10. If the build ___ (fail), we'll roll back.
11. What type of conditional is `If I were you, I'd ask the PM.`? Is it common in meetings?

**E. Reported speech**

12. Report it: Ken said, "I'm working on it."
13. Correct it: He said me that it's fine.
14. Correct it: She asked when will it be ready.
15. Correct it: Priya suggested us to wait.

**F. Spoken grammar**

16. Complete the fragment: Sounds good.
17. What signal does `actually` send in `Actually, it's not a bug.`?
18. In the subtitle `Let's move to the next face.`, what is the real word for `face`?
19. Clean the subtitle and write the skeleton: So we, we can, I mean, we should merge it today, right?

**G. Advanced grammar**

20. Rewrite with `What we need is…`: We need a clear owner.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. I'm not sure what the problem is. (statement order) → G18
2. if / whether → G18
3. I think we can leave it for now. → G18
4. that / which, or nothing → G19
5. who → G19
6. where → G19
7. Once → G20
8. Once it's ready, I'll tell you. → G20
9. until (`unless` also works: no release unless QA signs off) → G20
10. fails → G21
11. Second conditional (an unreal situation). Rare in meetings, 🟢 recognize only; for advice people usually say `Maybe you could ask the PM.` → G21
12. Ken said he's working on it. (still true, no backshift) or Ken said he was working on it. → G22
13. He told me that it's fine. or He said that it's fine. → G22
14. She asked when it will be ready. → G22
15. Priya suggested that we wait. or Priya suggested waiting. → G22
16. That sounds good. → G23
17. A correction or new information: "in fact…" → G23
18. phase → G23
19. We should merge it today, right? (take the version after `I mean`) → G23
20. What we need is a clear owner. → G24

</details>

## Final Test: Meeting Usage (M01–M07)

Situation questions have no single answer. Any answer with the right meaning and a suitable tone earns points.

**M01 Stand-ups**

1. Give a three-sentence stand-up update: you finished the login page yesterday; today you'll write unit tests; you have no blockers.
2. You are called on but have no update. What do you say?
3. Say that you're still working on the CI issue and it should be done by tomorrow.

**M02 Sprint sync**

4. The story is ___ track and should be done by Friday.
5. Say that this story will move into the next sprint unfinished.
6. Rewrite the subtitle: `two subtask are completed already and the rest is in progress.`

**M03 Hosting and screen sharing**

7. You've started sharing your screen. How do you check that everyone can see it?
8. You've finished the web part and want to start the native part. What do you say?
9. Invite Ken to give his part next.

**M04 Asking and clarifying**

10. You didn't catch the speaker's last sentence. What do you say?
11. Check your understanding: did they say the release is on Thursday?
12. Someone used a term you don't know, `canary release`. How do you ask?

**M05 Opinions and suggestions**

13. Disagree politely: you think the solution is too complex.
14. Suggest trying it in the test environment first.
15. The other person's explanation sounds reasonable. How do you respond?

**M06 Blockers, risks, and help**

16. Say you are blocked: you're waiting for the platform team to approve your access.
17. Ask someone to help you look at a build failure.
18. Raise a risk: the feature might not be finished by Friday.

**M07 Wrap-up and action items**

19. Before the meeting ends, confirm that Ken will update the ticket by Friday.
20. As the host, close the meeting.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Yesterday I finished the login page. Today I'm going to write the unit tests. No blockers. → M01
2. Nothing from my side. / No updates from me today. → M01
3. I'm still working on the CI issue. It should be done by tomorrow. → M01
4. on → M02
5. This story will carry over to the next sprint. → M02
6. Two subtasks are already completed, and the rest are in progress. → M02
7. Can everyone see my screen? / Is my screen visible? → M03
8. That's all for the web part. Let's move on to the native part. → M03
9. Ken, would you like to go next? / Ken, go ahead. → M03
10. Sorry, I didn't catch the last part. Could you say it again? → M04
11. So the release is on Thursday, right? / Just to confirm, you mean we release on Thursday? → M04
12. Sorry, what do you mean by "canary release"? → M04
13. I see your point, but I think this solution might be a bit too complex. → M05
14. How about trying it in the test environment first? / Maybe we could try it in the test environment first. → M05
15. That makes sense. / Good point. → M05
16. I'm blocked by the access approval from the platform team. / I'm waiting for the platform team to approve my access. → M06
17. Could someone help me take a look at this build failure? → M06
18. There's a risk that we won't finish this feature by Friday. → M06
19. So Ken will update the ticket by Friday, right? → M07
20. That's all for today. Thanks, everyone. → M07

</details>

## After the Tests

- **You passed all four stages:** your grammar base is enough. Focus on [P01 Subtitle Reading Drills](01-subtitle-reading-drills.md) and give one complete spoken update in every real meeting.
- **You didn't pass the final test:** reread M01 and M07 first — stand-up updates and wrap-ups are the two most common moments.
- Retake all the tests two weeks later and see which mistakes keep coming back.
